Dolly Parton’s Threads: My Songs in Symphony Comes to Heinz Hall
with the Pittsburgh Symphony on January 26
Announced on “9 to 5” Day, tickets go on sale Wednesday, September 30, and $1 from every ticket
supports Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library.
PITTSBURGH, PA— The Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra (PSO) marks 9/25, a date Dolly Parton fans
know well, by announcing Dolly Parton's Threads: My Songs in Symphony, coming to Heinz Hall on
Tuesday, January 26, 2027, at 7:30 p.m.
Threads: My Songs in Symphony celebrates the life and legacy of Dolly Parton in an innovative
multimedia experience featuring Dolly on screen, leading audiences in a visual-musical journey of her
songs, her life, and her stories. The Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra and a cast of guest vocalists and
musicians perform orchestrations by David Hamilton. The program includes "Jolene," "Coat of Many
Colors," and "I Will Always Love You," along with some of Dolly's personal favorites and a selection from
her upcoming Broadway musical.
The Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra has partnered with the Dollywood Foundation so that $1 from
every ticket sold supports Dolly Parton's Imagination Library. The program mails free books to children
from birth to age five.
Threads: My Songs in Symphony was co-produced by Dolly Parton together with Schirmer Theatrical and
Sony Music Publishing.

TICKETS
Tickets go on sale Wednesday, September 30 at 9:00AM and are available at pittsburghsymphony.org or
through the Heinz Hall Box Office at 412-392-4900.
ABOUT THE PITTSBURGH SYMPHONY ORCHESTRA
Now in its 130th year, the internationally acclaimed Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra (PSO) engages
audiences of all ages through unparalleled live musical experiences, community collaborations, and
educational programming that enrich every life with great music. The GRAMMY® Award-winning
symphony has been led by globally acclaimed Music Director Manfred Honeck since 2008. Past music
directors have included many of the greatest conductors of the twentieth century, including Fritz Reiner,
William Steinberg, André Previn, Lorin Maazel, and Mariss Jansons. The Pittsburgh Symphony has a long
and illustrious history in the areas of recordings and live radio broadcasts dating back to the 1930s.
Lauded as the Pittsburgh region’s international cultural ambassador, the orchestra began touring in
1896 and has embarked on scores of domestic and international tours. Heinz Hall, PSO’s home located
at 600 Penn Avenue in downtown Pittsburgh, is also owned and operated by the Pittsburgh Symphony.
Follow the Pittsburgh Symphony online at pittsburghsymphony.org, on Facebook, on Instagram, and on
YouTube.
