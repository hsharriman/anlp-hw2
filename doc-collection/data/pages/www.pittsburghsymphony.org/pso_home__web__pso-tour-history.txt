PSO Touring History
The Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra's first international tour occurred in 1947 with concerts in Mexico, under the baton of Fritz Reiner. It has since toured internationally in every decade except the 1950s, presenting concerts in major music halls in Africa (Canary Islands), Asia, Australia, Europe, Puerto Rico and South America.
The list of renowned conductors who have toured with the orchestra are (in order, historically): Fritz Reiner, William Steinberg, Donald Johanos, André Previn, Eduardo Mata, Rafael Frühbeck de Burgos, Herbert Blomstedt, Lorin Maazel, Michael Tilson Thomas, Mariss Jansons, Gilbert Levine, Leonard Slatkin, Hans Graf and Manfred Honeck.
In 1964, the Pittsburgh Symphony, under the baton of William Steinberg, was chosen by the United States Department of State to represent the nation on an 80-day, 40-concert tour of 27 cities in 14 countries in Europe and the Middle East to engage artists in cultural diplomacy.
In 2004, the Pittsburgh Symphony, under the baton of Gilbert Levine, was the first American orchestra to perform before Pope John Paul II at the Vatican.
The 2026 European Tour (August 26-September 10) is the PSO's 45th international tour and Manfred Honeck's 14th with the orchestra; his first was a tour of Asia in 2009.
International and selected domestic touring milestones
2026
August 26-September 10
European Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Announced three-week tour of 13 concerts in six countries and nine cities, including two performances at Lucerne Festival, Elbphilharmonie in Hamburg, the Chopin and His Europe Festival in Warsaw, and the Salzburg Festival.
2025
December 3
Carnegie Hall
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Return to Carnegie Hall, presented as part of Carnegie Hall's Great American Orchestras series, with pianist Seong-Jin Cho.
2024
August 22-September 7
European Festivals Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Nine-city tour of Austria, Italy and Germany, beginning as the only American orchestra at the Salzburg Festival and concluding as the opening concert of the Vienna Konzerthaus 2024-25 season.
2022
August 18-September 4
European Festivals Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Return to international touring, marking the PSO's 75th year of international tours. 11 concerts in Slovenia, Austria and Germany, beginning at the 70th Ljubljana Festival and concluding at Cologne Philharmonie.
2019
October 25-November 8
25th European Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. 11 concerts in 10 cities across five countries, including Frankfurt, Hamburg, Hannover, Berlin, Vienna, Munich, Amsterdam, Paris, Brussels and Düsseldorf.
2019
May 19
Lincoln Center Great Performers Series
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Return to Lincoln Center's David Geffen Hall, following a Pittsburgh send-off concert at Heinz Hall.
2017
August 25-September 9
European Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. The PSO's 24th European tour, to Germany, Austria, England, Switzerland and Romania. Included Rheingau Festival, Salzburg Festival, Grafenegg Festival, Lucerne Festival and the George Enescu Festival.
2016
May 17-June 5
European Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. 14-concert European tour to Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Belgium.
2014
May 10
Spring for Music Festival, Carnegie Hall
Manfred Honeck, conductor. The PSO closed the 2014 Spring for Music Festival at Carnegie Hall with Mozart's Requiem: Mozart's Death in Words and Music.
2013
August 29-September 14
European Festivals Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Concerts in Austria, Germany, France, Romania and Switzerland, including Grafenegg Festival, Musikfest Berlin, Beethoven Festival, George Enescu Festival and Lucerne Festival.
2012
October 26-November 10
European Residency Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Concerts in Barcelona, Madrid, Vienna, Paris, Cologne, Frankfurt, Stuttgart and Luxembourg, including a four-concert residency at the Musikverein.
2012
July 23-24
Lanaudière Festival
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Performances in Quebec, Canada.
2011
August 22-September 12
European Festivals Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Concerts in Germany, Lithuania, Austria, Switzerland, England and France, including the Rheingau Musik Festival, Schleswig-Holstein Musik Festival, Beethoven Festival, Musikfest Berlin, Grafenegg Festival, Lucerne Festival and the BBC Proms.
2010
July 23-24
Lanaudière Festival
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Performances in Quebec, Canada.
2010
May 12-30
European Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. Concerts in Basel, Stuttgart, Paris, Frankfurt, Luxembourg, Prague, Dresden, Vienna, Budapest and Ljubljana.
2009
September 12-20
European Festivals Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. First European tour with Honeck, with concerts for the Beethoven Festival and the close of the Lucerne Festival, plus concerts in Essen and Bonn.
2009
May 11-21
Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra Asia Tour
Manfred Honeck, conductor. First international tour with Music Director Manfred Honeck. First trip to Beijing in 22 years and PSO debuts in Shanghai and Kaohsiung.
2008
January, February
Pittsburgh 250 Ambassador Tour of Europe
Marek Janowski and Rafael Frühbeck de Burgos, conductors. The PSO kicked off the year-long celebration of Pittsburgh's 250th anniversary, accompanied by the Allegheny Conference. Tour countries included Spain, The Netherlands, Germany, Austria, Hungary and Croatia.
2006
August 22-September 6
European Tour
Leonard Slatkin and Hans Graf, conductors. Concerts in Greece, Ireland, Wales, England and Germany; the August 25 concert in Patras took place during the city's year as European Capital of Culture.
2004
January 17
The Pontiff's Silver Jubilee Celebration
Gilbert Levine, conductor. First American orchestra to perform before Pope John Paul II at the Vatican, commemorating the 25th anniversary of the Pope's election to the papacy.
2003
August 16-31
Mellon Pittsburgh Symphony European Tour
Mariss Jansons, conductor. Jansons' last tour as PSO music director, with festival concerts in Lucerne, Salzburg, Stuttgart and at the London Proms.
2003
March 31-April 14
Mellon Pittsburgh Symphony European Tour
Mariss Jansons, conductor. Sponsored by Mellon Financial Corporation with additional support from FreeMarkets. Cities included Valencia, Madrid, Vienna, Amsterdam and London.
2002
February 18-March 9
Far East Tour
Mariss Jansons, conductor. Second trip to Japan under Jansons, and first PSO appearances in Malaysia and Australia.
2001
June 3-23
South American Tour
Mariss Jansons, conductor. First PSO South American tour under Jansons, with concerts in Sao Paulo, Montevideo and Buenos Aires.
2000
May 15-June 4
European Residency Tour
Mariss Jansons, conductor. 14 concerts in Madrid, Valencia, Amsterdam, Brussels, Vienna, London and Birmingham.
1999
August 12-September 5
European Festivals Tour
Mariss Jansons, conductor. First PSO European tour under Jansons. 15 concerts in 13 cities, including Dublin, Edinburgh, Salzburg, Copenhagen, London, Frankfurt, Wiesbaden, Lucerne, Baden-Baden, Stuttgart, Cologne, Berlin and Düsseldorf.
1998
U.S. and Canada with Andrea Bocelli
Tour appearances with Andrea Bocelli.
1998
May 16-28
Tour of Japan
Mariss Jansons, conductor. The world's introduction to the PSO with Jansons as music director. Seven concerts in five cities: Sapporo, Nagoya, Himeji, Osaka and Tokyo.
1996
January 29-February 22
International Centennial Tour
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Part of the PSO's 100th anniversary celebration, with debut performances in the Canary Islands and Israel and participation in Jerusalem's 3,000th anniversary celebration.
1995
Casals Festival in Puerto Rico
Lorin Maazel, conductor.
1995
May 16-June 4
Tour of Japan and Korea
Lorin Maazel, conductor. First PSO concert in Korea. Benefit concert in Japan's Kobe Green Arena for victims of the January 1995 earthquake.
1994
August 14-September 10
Tour of International Summer Music Festivals
Lorin Maazel, conductor. 21 concerts in nine countries, including debut performances at Tanglewood and in the Gewandhaus in Leipzig.
1993
May 24-June 9
Tour to South America
Lorin Maazel, conductor. The PSO's first South American tour, with concerts in Argentina, Brazil and Venezuela.
1993
April 25-May 9
Tour to California and Mexico
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Opening concerts of the Chivas Regal Latin American Tour in Mexico City.
1992
August 5-29
European Tour to Major Summer Music Festivals
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Concerts in Spain, Germany, Switzerland and Italy, including Santander, Seville, Wiesbaden, Frankfurt, Montreux, Stresa, Verona and Torino.
1992
May 18-June 9
European Tour to Major Music Capitals
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Cities included Hamburg, Munich, Frankfurt, Paris, Madrid, Barcelona, Vienna, Stuttgart, Bonn, Brussels, Birmingham and London.
1991
May 14-June 5
Tour of Japan, Hong Kong and Taiwan
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Debut in Taiwan. Cities included Tokyo, Omiya, Osaka, Musashino, Hong Kong and Taipei.
1989
October 2-28
Tour of Soviet Union, Poland and Western Europe
Lorin Maazel, conductor. European premieres of Marc Neikrug's Flute Concerto and George Rochberg's Symphony No. 6. Cities included Leningrad, Moscow, Warsaw, Geneva, Paris, Milan, Rome, Amsterdam, Cologne, Berlin, Hamburg, Hanover and London.
1987
Edinburgh Festival
Lorin Maazel and Michael Tilson Thomas, conductors. The PSO was the first American orchestra designated Resident Orchestra at the Edinburgh Festival.
1987
April 14-May 4
Far East Tour
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Historic visit to Beijing, China: the first U.S. orchestra to visit in the 1980s and only the third ever to do so. Cities included Tokyo, Yokohama, Osaka, Nagoya, Matsudo, Hong Kong and Beijing.
1985
August 14-September 8
European Tour
Lorin Maazel, conductor. Included the Salzburg and Edinburgh festivals, with concerts in Ireland, England, Switzerland, Austria, Germany, Belgium and France.
1984
Casals Festival in Puerto Rico
Herbert Blomstedt, conductor.
1984
Hong Kong Festival
André Previn, conductor.
1982
May 23-June 13
European Tour
André Previn, conductor. Cities included Bonn, Linz, Vienna, Zurich, Munich, Stuttgart, Frankfurt, Dusseldorf, Paris, Brussels, Berlin and London.
1980
August 25-31
Mexico City
Eduardo Mata and Rafael Frühbeck de Burgos, conductors. Four concerts in Mexico City.
1978
May 21-June 10
European Tour
André Previn, conductor. Cities in Austria, Germany, Norway, Sweden and England, including Vienna, Munich, Frankfurt, Berlin, Bergen, Stockholm and London.
1973
Tour of Japan, Alaska and Oregon
William Steinberg and Donald Johanos, conductors.
1964
August 10-November 1
Tour of Europe and Near East
William Steinberg, conductor. State Department-funded three-month tour of Europe and what was then referred to as the Near East. Cities included Rome, Athens, Beirut, Baalbeck, Tehran, Lucerne, Edinburgh, Luxembourg, Frankfurt, Berlin, Warsaw, Krakow, Katowice, Lodz, Belgrade, Sarajevo, Ljubljana, Zagreb, Munich, Milan, Turin, Florence, Bilbao, Madrid, Barcelona, Lisbon, Oporto and Reykjavik.
1947
Tour of Mexico
Fritz Reiner, conductor.
