BRAVO! Champions of Music Education
In this ever-changing climate, music education can be a powerful tool for building community, encouraging self-expression, and fostering resilience. This season, the Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra will recognize champions in music education who provide high quality music learning opportunities and broad access to students throughout the region. This includes the essential behind-the-scenes advocates who create school schedules, safe spaces, creative environments, and seamless logistics that enable students to flourish.
This new program, BRAVO! Champions for Music Education, will turn the spotlight on individuals in various roles who work to promote music learning in school-based music programs for PreK through 12th grade students.

2026-27 Honorees
Katie Black
Orchestra Director, Butler Senior High & Intermediate High School, Butler Area School District
Katie Black is an orchestra educator and violinist who believes music has the power to create community, build confidence, and give students a place to belong. She has taught in the Butler Area School District since 2015, working with students in grades 6–12. She is the founder and director of Butler Rock Orchestra, an audition-based group that takes classical strings and combines rock elements. Under her direction, the ensemble has performed throughout the region, including at the PMEA State Conference in 2020 and 2024, various elementary tours, and last December performed a sold out show in collaboration with Cello Fury at the historic Penn Theater in Butler.
Katie is passionate about creating meaningful opportunities for students to perform, lead, and find their own voice through music. She also performs throughout the region as a professional violinist and fiddler with The Jive Daisy and other musical projects. She holds a Bachelor of Music Education from Seton Hill University. Above all, Katie believes music brings people together and creates meaningful memories and experiences that can shape students for years to come.
Karen Bottegal
Music Teacher, Pittsburgh Montessori PreK-5, Pittsburgh Public Schools
I am an elementary general music teacher with Pittsburgh Public Schools, currently teaching at Pittsburgh Montessori PreK-5. I believe music education is about so much more than learning to sing, play an instrument, or read music. Music can help students discover who they are and find a place where they belong. It gives them opportunities to express themselves, connect with others, build confidence, and experience the joy of creating something together.
I also believe music brings joy to education. For some students, music class is the place where they feel most successful, most comfortable being themselves, or simply most excited to come to school. I love watching students discover something in music that becomes theirs, whether that is singing, playing an instrument, composing, moving, performing, or just realizing that they are a musician.
One of the most important parts of my work is making sure those experiences are available to every child. I want my students to leave elementary school knowing that music is something they can participate in, enjoy, and carry with them, wherever their lives take them.
Eryn Carranza
Band Director, South Fayette High School and Intermediate School, South Fayette Twp. School District
Eryn Carranza began her tenure with the South Fayette Township School District in 2007 and has served as Director of Bands at South Fayette High School since 2011. In this role, she conducts the marching band, concert bands, pep band, pit orchestra, percussion ensemble, and, most recently, the jazz ensemble. She also teaches at South Fayette Intermediate School, where she directs the Beginning Band and 5th Grade Band, giving her the unique opportunity to work with students from their very first experiences as instrumental musicians through their high school years.
From 2019 through 2025, Eryn had the honor of serving the members of District 1 of the Pennsylvania Music Educators Association (PMEA), including as District President from 2021 to 2023. During a period marked by the challenges of the pandemic, she helped reestablish in-person PMEA events, establish a mini-grant program to support music education throughout the district, and strengthen connections among music educators across the region.
Eryn continues to perform on her primary instrument, tuba, with community ensembles including the Allegheny Brass Band and West Hills Symphonic Band. She is also a proud two-time member of the Saluting America’s Band Directors Marching Band, performing in both the Rose Parade and Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade.
Eryn believes music is at the core of who we are as human beings. It is an essential part of our culture and our humanity, with a remarkable power to unite people across generations, cultures, and differences. Music can make us rejoice, make us weep, and help us express what words alone cannot. In a world that continues to yearn for peace, music and the arts offer hope for greater unity, acceptance, and understanding.
So many of the people who have shaped Eryn’s life are in it because of music education. Her hope is to provide that same gift to her students—not only by teaching them to become musicians, but by creating a place where they feel safe, where they belong, where they are valued and loved, and where they are free to become the truest versions of themselves.
