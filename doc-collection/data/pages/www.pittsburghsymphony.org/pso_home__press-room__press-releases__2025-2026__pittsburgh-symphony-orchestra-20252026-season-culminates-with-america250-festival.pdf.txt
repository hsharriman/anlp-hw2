Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra 2025/2026 Season
Culminates with America250 Festival
Two-week festival celebrates American music, Pittsburgh partnerships,
and the United States' 250th Anniversary
June 12 & 14: Academy Award-winning actor F. Murray Abraham joins the PSO
to narrate Copland’s Lincoln Portrait
June 19-21: Pittsburgh Ballet Theatre collaborates on Copland’s Appalachian Spring,
paired with world premiere of Michael Daugherty’s Dancing in the Streets
July 2-5: PSO Celebrates America250 with free concerts and a new recording
PITTSBURGH – The Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra (PSO) concludes its 2025/2026 BNY
Classics season with an America250 Festival, a two-week celebration of American music in
honor of America’s 250th led by Music Director Manfred Honeck at Heinz Hall. Presented
across the final two subscription weekends of the season, the festival brings together landmark
American works, a PSO-commissioned world premiere, major guest artists, and two Pittsburgh
partnerships: the Mendelssohn Choir of Pittsburgh and a mainstage collaboration together with
Pittsburgh Ballet Theatre.
The festival is the culminating expression of the PSO’s 2025/2026 season theme, which has
explored the evolving role of American orchestral music in the nation’s 250th year. Across the
season, the PSO’s classical programming has featured 21 works by American composers,
including music by Leonard Bernstein, Aaron Copland, Samuel Barber, George Gershwin, Julia
Wolfe, Jessie Montgomery, Caroline Shaw, Carlos Simon, and many others.
“Throughout this season, we’ve explored American music not as a single sound, but as a living
and evolving conversation shaped by an incredible variety of voices, communities, and
traditions,” said Melia Tourangeau, President & CEO of the PSO. “Our America250 Festival
brings that journey to a powerful culmination by celebrating American composers past and
present, while honoring the special creative ties that make Pittsburgh such a distinctive cultural
home.”

June 12 & 14: Lincoln Portrait
The concerts on June 12 & 14 feature Academy Award-winning actor F. Murray Abraham as
narrator in Aaron Copland’s Lincoln Portrait, a work that frames the words of Abraham Lincoln

as Copland saw them. Abraham returns to the PSO following several previous collaborations,
including his narration of Manfred Honeck’s concept Mozart’s Death in Words and Music,
recently released as an album, and appearances with the orchestra at Carnegie Hall in 2014 and
Heinz Hall in 2023.
The program opens with Joan Tower’s Tambor, a work commissioned and premiered by the PSO
in 1988 under Mariss Jansons. The concert also features the PSO premiere of John Adams’ The
Wound-Dresser, a moving setting of Walt Whitman’s Civil War poem about tending to injured
soldiers, brought to life by German baritone Matthias Goerne. The PSO is joined by the
Mendelssohn Choir of Pittsburgh and boy soprano Hudson Schmitt for Leonard Bernstein’s
lively Chichester Psalms.
____________________________________________
June 19-21: Appalachian Spring
The season finale centers on the PSO’s largescale collaboration with Pittsburgh Ballet Theatre,
who steps center stage for Aaron Copland’s beloved Appalachian Spring. PBT will perform
Mark Godden’s Angels in the Architecture choreography in front of the orchestra, bringing
dance and live orchestral music together in PSO’s ending to the BNY Classics season.
Appalachian Spring carries several resonances for Pittsburgh: the ballet was originally created
for Martha Graham, who was born in Pittsburgh and became one of the defining figures of
modern dance. Pittsburgh itself is also situated in Appalachia, lending a regional connection to a
work that has become one of the most recognizable expressions of American musical identity.
The program opens with the world premiere of Michael Daugherty’s Dancing in the Streets, a
PSO commission written in celebration of America’s 250th anniversary and the 100th
anniversary of the Martha Graham Dance Company. The three-movement work continues the
program’s dance themes through musical tributes to four American cultural figures: Pittsburgh-
born Gene Kelly, Martha Graham, and Woody and Marjorie Guthrie.
Violinist Augustin Hadelich, widely recognized among the foremost violinists performing today,
joins the PSO for another nod to America with Samuel Barber’s Violin Concerto. Jessie
Montgomery’s colorful Strum for string orchestra adds a contemporary counterpoint to this
bouquet of American sounds and voices.

July 3-5: Parks Concerts and a Recording Release
July 2-5: Free Concerts and a Recording Release
The Pittsburgh Symphony is also marking America’s 250th with free concerts across July 2, 3, 4
and 5, with programs celebrating American music, civic pride and PSO talent. On July 2,
Associate Conductor Jacob Joyce leads the Symphony’s annual Americana Concert at Soldiers &

Sailors Memorial Hall & Museum, preceded by a flag-raising ceremony with the National Flag
Foundation’s Sojourn250.
The Symphony continues with free summer parks concerts on July 3 at South Park Amphitheatre
with Allegheny County, July 4 at the Dollar Bank Stage at Arts Landing as part of the City of
Pittsburgh’s Independence Day celebration with the Pittsburgh Cultural Trust, and July 5 at
Hartwood Acres Park as part of Allegheny County’s America’s 250th Anniversary Celebration.
Fittingly, the PSO will also release a new American-themed recording on July 3, the day before
Independence Day. Released on Reference Recordings, the album pairs Music Director Manfred
Honeck and the Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra in Dvořák’s Symphony No. 9, “From the New
World,” with the first commercial recording of Carlos Simon’s Four Black American Dances,
bringing together two perspectives on American music: Dvořák’s experience of America and
Simon’s vibrant contemporary voice.
