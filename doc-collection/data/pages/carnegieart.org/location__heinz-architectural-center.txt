Carnegie Museum of Art has commissioned award-winning art, architecture, and urban design practice AD-WO, led by Emanuel Admassu and Jen Wood, to reimagine the Heinz Architectural Center. Over the coming years, AD-WO will continue to collaborate with staff across Carnegie Museum of Art to shape the evolution of this renowned space.
Founded in 2018, AD-WO has earned international recognition for work that bridges architecture, exhibition design, urbanism, and cultural research. Their practice explores relationships among people, place, history, and material culture, bringing a thoughtful, interdisciplinary approach to how we experience and understand architecture. This perspective makes them a natural partner for the Heinz Architectural Center’s next chapter.
The Center will transform into a forward-looking hub for exhibitions, scholarship, and community, making its work more porous through new gallery spaces, a collection study center, and infrastructure that supports stronger connections between curatorial research and the visitor experience. The project will create new opportunities for visitors, scholars, students, and practitioners to engage with architecture and explore its many ways of shaping contemporary life.
The reimagined Heinz Architectural Center fosters openness, discovery, and exchange. At the heart of the museum’s 1907 building, the Center sits between its historic halls and the Scaife Galleries, designed by Edward Larrabee Barnes and completed in 1974. The new design builds on this location, strengthening connections across the museum while bringing together exhibitions, collections, research, learning, and public engagement.
Greater access to natural light and a material palette inspired by Pittsburgh’s geological and industrial histories, including firebrick and aluminum, will root the Center in the region’s distinctive character. The project will also reimagine the existing collection study center and create flexible gallery spaces supporting exhibitions, education, research, and public programs. Together, these spaces will open the Center’s collections more fully to students, scholars, practitioners, and the public.
About Heinz Architectural Center
Established in 1993, the Heinz Architectural Center at Carnegie Museum of Art is a forum for the presentation, exploration, and incubation of architectural ideas and issues of regional, national, and international significance. Through exhibitions, collections, research, publications, and public programs, the Center examines architecture and the built environment across histories, geographies, and perspectives.
Created through the generosity of Drue Heinz, the Center presents exhibitions organized by Carnegie Museum of Art curators as well as exhibitions originating at other institutions. Its program reexamines architectural history, investigates the present, and considers possible futures for architecture and the built environment.
Heinz Architectural Center’s collection encompasses approximately 6,000 architecture-related objects and continues to grow as a resource for scholarly research and public display.
These holdings are part of Carnegie Museum of Art’s broader collection of more than 100,000 objects, which spans painting and sculpture; prints, drawings, and photographs; architectural casts, renderings, and models; decorative arts and design; time-based and digital media; and installations.
