Torkwase Dyson, Tomorrow Was Yesterday
Screenings May 3, 2026–Jan. 3, 2027
Installation view of Torkwase Dyson, Tomorrow Was Yesterday (2026), Kamin Science Center, presenting partner of If the word we, 59th Carnegie International, Carnegie Museum of Art, Pittsburgh; photo: Zachary Riggleman / © Carnegie Museum of Art, Pittsburgh
When
Wed. through Jan. 3, 4 p.m.
Sun. through Jan. 3, 4 p.m.
Where
Kamin Science Center
Tickets
Free
Tomorrow Was Yesterday is an animation informed by Torkwase Dyson’s ongoing research into oil and gas drilling in the Caribbean seabed. This program immerses viewers in an underwater environment conceptualized by the artist, where the machinery of the resource industry has taken hold.
Dyson’s explorations question: “What is the magnitude of oil and gas extraction infrastructure? Why is there an incomprehensible distance between our everyday lives and the extraction points sprawled across this planet’s seabeds? Seabed mining is a transhistorical issue and I center process as a way of understanding its history and scale.”
Registration
Between May 6, 2026, and January 3, 2027, experience the artwork on Wednesdays and select Sundays at 4 p.m. Staff members at the Kamin Science Center visitor services desk will direct you to the Buhl Planetarium.
Seating is first come, first served.

59th Carnegie International
Organized every four years by Carnegie Museum of Art, the Carnegie International is the longest-running exhibition of international art in North America. The 59th edition is the most collaborative and far-reaching to date—a clear expression of the museum’s founding commitment to the art and artists of our time, at once grounded in our locality and extending globally.
About Kamin Science Center
Kamin Science Center is dedicated to inspiring learning and curiosity by connecting science and technology with everyday life. One of the four Carnegie Museums, Kamin Science Center is Pittsburgh’s premier science exploration destination and the region’s most-visited museum, attracting more than 500,000 visitors each year through its hands-on exhibits, camps, classes, and education programs.
