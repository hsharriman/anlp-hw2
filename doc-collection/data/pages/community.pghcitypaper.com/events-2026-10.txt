We'll also have live music and a live painter (artist Phil Seth) to liven up the evening. About our Venue: The Mattress Factory is a contemporary art museum located in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. It was a pioneer of site-specific installation art and features permanent installations by artists Yayoi Kusama, James Turrell, and Greer Lankton. The museum's roof itself is a light art installation and part of Pittsburgh's Northside evening skyline. Barbara Luderowski purchased a derelict Stearns & Foster mattress warehouse in 1975. The museum achieved non-profit status in 1977. Over the next forty years, Luderowski would attract upcoming installation artists to fill its rooms. The Mattress Factory along with its neighbors City of Asylum and Randyland are credited with playing a role in Pittsburgh's revitalization. (SOURCE: Wikipedia)

Textures of Being Community Forum: Saturday, October 24, 2026, 9:30 AM – 3:30 PM, Contemporary Craft, 5645 Butler Street · Pittsburgh · Lawrenceville
Price: $20 ($12 for students), registration required.. Category: Arts & Community: Community, Arts & Community: Exhibits.
Textures of Being Forum is an intimate, daylong program centered on disability, accessibility, the body, and artistic practice. Textures of Being Community Forum is an intimate, daylong program presented through a collaboration between Contemporary Craft and Anthropology of Motherhood. Centered on disability, accessibility, the body, and artistic practice, the forum brings together artists, advocates, and cultural leaders to consider how access shapes the ways we communicate, create, organize, and participate in community life. The program will feature three complementary presentations. Artist and self-advocate Mark Steidl will address creative voice, communication, and the importance of disabled people telling their own stories. Kristen Link of The Woodlands Foundation will discuss how organizations can move beyond individual accommodations to build sustained cultures of access and belonging. Artists from the Anthropology of Motherhood: Deaf & Disability Art Exhibition will share how Deaf and disability culture, caregiving, embodiment, and access inform their artistic processes and relationships with audiences. Through presentations, facilitated conversation, and an interactive “Cards for Inclusion” activity, participants will explore accessibility not as an afterthought, but as a creative, relational, and institutional practice. The forum is designed for a small group of approximately 25 participants, allowing space for meaningful dialogue, shared learning, and connection. 1. Ten Evenings: Michael Lewis: Monday, October 26, 2026, 7:30 PM – 8:30 PM, Carnegie Music Hall, 4400 Forbes Avenue · Pittsburgh · Oakland
Price: $30.94 to $42.14. Category: Arts & Community: Lecture, Arts & Community: Literary.
An inside look at the early days of Trump’s Department of Government Efficiency, as told by the public servants it targeted and one of America’s best storytellers. Blockers. It wasn’t a compliment. In the new Washington, it was the worst thing you could be—especially if you worked in the federal government and wanted to keep your job. A Blocker was a holdover, an impediment, someone who didn’t get the message—and, most of all, someone who didn’t belong. Michael Lewis found seven of them. Blockers is the story of people who believed they had a calling, who were convinced they were doing something the country couldn’t do without, and then discovered they were no longer wanted. One kept Americans’ most sensitive tax data out of the wrong hands and another made sure that politicians and civil servants played by the rules. One figured out how to stop wildfires from destroying suburban neighborhoods, another helped uncover the mysteries of cystic fibrosis. There was also a security guard. It should come as no surprise that the writer who made subprime mortgages gripping (The Big Short) and who turned baseball statistics into poetry (Moneyball) could transform the first two years of the second Trump administration into a thriller about federal employees. But he does. And he does something else. He puts before us a clear question: If these public servants can’t survive in government today, then what does that say about us? New York Times bestselling author Michael Lewis has published many books on subjects ranging from politics to Wall Street. Lewis’s most recent #1 New York Times bestselling book, Going Infinite, tells the story of FTX’s collapse and the enigmatic founder at its center, Sam Bankman-Fried. Lewis is the creator and host of Apple’s #1 rated podcast Against the Rules, a searing look at what’s happened to fairness in American life through the lens of people who depend on public trust. Mr. Lewis grew up in New Orleans and remains deeply interested and involved in the city. He holds a Bachelor’s degree in Art History from Princeton and a Master’s degree in Economics from the London School of Economics. He lives in Berkeley, California, with his wife, Tabitha Soren, and their children.

The story of a brief, passionate, life-changing love affair, wrapped up in what might be the greatest romance of all: our eternal human longing for art and beauty.
