"Out of Body" Art Exhibition Opening: Friday, November 6, 2026, 6:00 PM – 9:00 PM, Irma Freeman Center for Imagination, 5006 Penn Ave. · Pittsburgh · Garfield
Price: free. Category: Arts & Community: Visual Art, Music: Indie.
In November of 2026, Irma Freeman Center for Imagination will be presenting a special project organized by artist Tom Sarver. Out of Body is a show of four Pittsburgh artists who approach the title theme from different directions. Maritza Mosquera creates visceral images that allude to one’s inner and outer connection to living, both physically and spiritually. Sarah Zeffiro creates large-scale abstract paintings that push the physicality of art making with the joy and exuberance of mark and symbol. Tom Sarver delves deep into the mind to render images of Pittsburgh arts experiences in specific time and place. David Montano’s conceptual approach often begins with the finding of a cherished artifact of the past, then responding with a work that contemplates the life of the original owner. Out of Body has roots in the recent publication, Pittsburgh’s Avant-Garde: 60 Years Inside the Underground Art Scene, by Sheila Ali, which featured work by Sarver and Zeffiro. It continues the avant-garde spirit of that project and brings new voices, Montano and Mosquera, into the conversation. A tribute to Sheila Ali, longtime curator and director of programs at the Irma Freeman Center of Imagination, will be part of the exhibition. Ami Taf Ra & Kamasi Washington presenting The Prophet & The Madman: Friday, November 6, 2026, 6:00 PM, City Winery Pittsburgh, 1627 Smallman Street · Pittsburgh · Strip District
Price: 66. Category: Music: Jazz, Music: Soul.
Presented in partnership with City Winery Pittsburgh North African, Los Angeles-based vocalist and composer Ami Taf Ra joins acclaimed saxophonist, composer, and producer Kamasi Washington for a special November 2026 tour presentation celebrating their ongoing creative collaboration and Ami’s debut album, The Prophet and the Madman. Produced by Washington and released through Brainfeeder, The Prophet and the Madman brings together Moroccan Gnawa, Arabic musical traditions, gospel, soul, and spiritual jazz in a deeply personal exploration of duality, healing, ancestral memory, and transformation. Inspired in part by the writings of Lebanese-American poet Kahlil Gibran, the album creates a sweeping musical world in which Ami’s commanding, operatic voice moves between intimacy, theatricality, and spiritual release. Since its release, the album has earned significant critical recognition. The Prophet and the Madman is nominated for Best Spiritual Album at the Libera Awards, and the Ami/Washington collaboration “Gnawa” was named one of NPR’s Best Songs of 2025. Ami Taf Ra was also recognized among Stereogum’s Best New Artists of 2025. The artistic connection between Ami and Washington is at the heart of the project. Their collaboration can be heard on “How I Became a Madman,” a soaring meditation on the tension between inner chaos and clarity, and throughout an album shaped by Washington’s expansive production and Ami’s singular cross-cultural perspective. Together, they create music that feels both ancient and forward-looking, connecting North African and Middle Eastern traditions with the freedom, improvisation, and communal spirit of contemporary jazz. Ami has performed internationally across Europe, North Africa, the Middle East, and the United States, including appearances with the Dutch Royal Metropole Orchestra, North Sea Jazz, Blue Note Tokyo, and Ronnie Scott’s in the UK, as well as performances at the Hollywood Bowl Jazz Festival, City Winery Boston, and the Just Jazz Concert Series in Los Angeles. Grammy winning and Emmy nominated composer and saxophonist Kamasi Washington is one of the defining musical voices of his generation, building an expansive body of work that reaches across jazz, soul, hip-hop, classical music, film, and TV. Their November 2026 performances offer audiences a rare opportunity to experience the world of The Prophet and the Madman live, with Ami’s evocative storytelling and extraordinary vocal range meeting the power, imagination, and spiritual intensity of Kamasi Washington’s musical universe.

About Greer Lankton Greer Lankton (1958 – 1996) was one of the most significant artists to have taken part in the revolutionary art scene of New York City’s East Village during the 1980s. Lankton grew up in Park Forest, IL, where she graduated a year early from high school to attend the Art Institute of Chicago from 1975 to1978. That year she moved to New York City and received her BFA from Pratt Institute in 1981. By then Lankton had secured her reputation as a leading figure in the social ferment of NYC in the 1980s through her visceral doll sculpture, and now lesser-known performances and minimalist soft sculpture. Lankton’s drawings are frequently compared to that of the expressionist Egon Schiele and her dolls and their photographic portraits, to that of surrealist Hans Bellmer. Yet her experimental and multimedia work are located within a history of transfeminism, avant-garde performance and even pop-art, while at the same time is equally situated within a neo-punk canon and the Trash Trilogy of John Waters films - Pink Flamingos being her favorite. Lankton’s exhibitions and performances included those at PS1, Club 57, Pyramid Club, Franklyn Furnace, Civilian Warfare Gallery, Hal Bromm and the Whitney Biennale, NYC. She also exhibited across the US and Europe, including the UK, Austria and the Venice Biennale, Italy. She exhibited her first full-scale installation artwork at the Mattress Factory Museum shortly before her untimely death in 1996. About the monograph Could It Be Love Edited by Nan Goldin, Francis Schichtel, and Jordan Weitzman, the book draws deeply from the Greer Lankton Archive, housed at Mattress Factory, to present over 100 of Lankton’s rarely seen photographs. These images offer an intimate and theatrical glimpse into Lankton’s inner world, one populated by glamorous oddballs, downtown legends, and avatars of her own making.
