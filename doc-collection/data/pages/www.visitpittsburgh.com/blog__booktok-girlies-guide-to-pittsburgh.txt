Between the Pages: A Bookish Guide to Pittsburgh
Paging all book lovers!
With the rise of things like BookTok and Bookstagram, more and more people are discovering or rediscovering their love of books and stories. Luckily, Pittsburgh has been picking up traction in the book scene for years, with book clubs and events throughout the city designed to make you feel at home with other book lovers.

Annual Events & Things To Do
PGH Book Fest
Established in 2022, Pgh Book Fest is chock-full of authors, poets, events and workshops for readers of all ages! Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh's Oakland branch hosts the Pgh Book Fest, and it's truly the perfect hub for all things literary. The Festival is FREE and brings together readers of all ages for a day of author talks, poetry readings, workshops, local booksellers, literary exhibitors and vendors, and much more.

The Great Pittsburgh Book Crawl
The Great Pittsburgh Book Crawl is held once a year, with dozens of participating stores scattered throughout the city and beyond. A book crawl encourages you to stop and visit participating stores during the dates of the crawl and shop their inventory. At each stop you can get a passport book stamped showing that you have visited that location (passport books are available for free at each participating location). Get enough stamps and you’ll be entered to win a raffle prize put together by all the stores.

Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh | 4400 Forbes Avenue (Oakland)
With more than 2 million visitors and 3.4 million items borrowed each year, Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh is the region' s most visited asset and one of the largest public libraries in Pennsylvania. Visit the main campus in Oakland or the several branches located throughout the city.
The Andy Warhol Museum | 117 Sandusky Street (North Side)
The Warhol is one of the most comprehensive single-artist museums in the world and the largest in North America. At The Warhol you have the chance to truly experience art. Whether you come to see our permanent collection, a performance, a visiting artist’s work, or a party, you’ll find something to talk about, chuckle over, delight in, or ponder for days.
Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens | 1 Schenley Drive (Oakland)
Explore the beauty and wonders of nature at Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens, encompassing 15 acres including the historic 14-room glasshouse and 23 distinct gardens. Experience breathtaking seasonal flower shows, exclusive commissioned exhibits, immersive Tropical Forest, renowned orchid and bonsai collections, industry-leading sustainable architecture and green practices, delightful butterflies, fun family activities and much more.
The Frick Pittsburgh Museums & Gardens | 7227 Reynolds Street (Point Breeze)
The Frick Pittsburgh is a fascinating complex of museums and historical buildings located on nearly six acres of lawns and gardens in Pittsburgh's residential East End. The Frick is devoted to the interpretation of the life and times of industrialist and art collector Henry Clay Frick. Exhibitions of fine and decorative art are also presented at the Frick.
Carnegie Museums of Art and Natural History | 4400 Forbes Avenue (Oakland)
Explore two world-class museums with one ticket at Carnegie Museums of Art and Natural History! Located in the Oakland neighborhood, the museums provide an experience filled with culture, creativity, and exploration to all who visit. Carnegie Museum of Art, renowned for its collections of more than 100,00 objects, features a range of visual art, including painting, sculpture, decorative arts, design, and photography. Located within the same historic building, Carnegie Museum of Natural History is home to one of the country’s largest and most celebrated natural history collections featuring 22 million objects and specimens, ranging from real dinosaur fossils and dazzling gems and minerals to iconic wildlife dioramas.
Pittsburgh Penguins | 1001 Fifth Avenue (Uptown)
The stories Pittsburgh Penguins play their home games at PPG Paints Arena and going to a game is a must for anyone visiting the city, including those who are fans of sports romance books.

Must-Visit Bookstores
White Whale | 4754 Liberty Avenue (Bloomfield)
In the running for USA Today's Best Independent Bookstore of 2026, White Whale is a neighborhood indie known for its thoughtfully curated shelves and strong focus on small-press and independent titles. It's one-half bookstore and one-half coffee shop and cafe that feels like home the second you step inside.
Passionate Pages | 4304 Butler Street (Lawrenceville)
Pittsburgh's first romance-only bookstore, Passionate Pages opened in the beginning of 2026 and has quickly become a staple to the community. With an aesthetic storefront, welcoming community, and built-in book club, this bookstore will quickly steal your heart.
Shelf Love Society | 4745 Butler Street (Lawrenceville)
This queer, woman-owned romance-only bookstore can be found right across the street from Allegheny Cemetery. While Pittsburgh's other romance-exclusive bookstore, Passionate Pages, focuses more on romantasy and popular romance titles, The Shelf Love Society puts the spotlight on diverse, queer, and indie voices.
City of Asylum Bookstore | 40 W North Avenue (North Side)
This bookstore has something for everyone. One-part bookstore, one-part restaurants, one-part dedicated writer space, and one-part performance space, City of Asylum is a haven for those looking to dive between the pages of their current read, or write their own story others can dive into instead.
Little Green Bookstore |104 N Main Street (Zelienople)
Little Green Bookstore is around 40 minutes north of the city, but it's so worth the drive. This used bookstore is tucked away in the quaint town of Zelienople, offering a natural pause to your walk through the downtown area.
Blythe Books & Gifts | 2941 Brownsville Road (Brentwood)
The South Hills' premier used bookstore, Blythe is a staple in Pittsburgh's bookish community. From founding the Great Pittsburgh Book Crawl to hosting a variety of authors both local and national, this cute bookstore is a must-visit during your Pittsburgh adventure.
Posman Books | 1637 Smallman Street (Strip District)
Posman Books is an independent bookstore built by book lovers, offering a thoughtfully curated selection of books, stationery, gifts, and unique treasures for every type of shopper. Whether you're searching for a bestselling novel, a timeless classic, a locally inspired gift, or the perfect greeting card, every visit is filled with opportunities for discovery.
Woolly Bear Books and Gifts | 31 E Main Street (Carnegie)
A queer-owned independent bookstore in Carnegie, a quick drive just south of the city, Woolly Bear got its name because owner Mike Ploetz wanted a mascot he could stick a smiley face on. If that doesn't tell you everything you need to know about how cute this store is, we don't know what will.
Amazing Books and Records | 5858 Forbes Avenue (Squirrel Hill) & 320 Smithfield Street (Downtown)
A beloved Pittsburgh bookstore, Amazing Books and Records frequently hosts special promotional nights, especially during various holidays. Plus, for every three book or record you buy, you get a free one
Riverstone Books | 5841 Forbes Avenue (Squirrel Hill) & 8850 Covenant Avenue (McCandless)
Riverstone Books is the largest independent, general interest bookstore serving the readers in the northern communities of Pittsburgh. Hosting frequent signings by best-selling authors, Riverstone is a must-visit bookstore and is worth the drive.

Book Clubs You Should Join or Stop By
Silent Book Club | Various
This is the book club that you can find this author at every month. Designed like silent reading time when you were in school, this book club is perfect for those who don't like assigned reading. At Silent Book Club, you're encouraged to bring whatever book you want in whatever medium you want - that includes audiobooks, comic books, Kindles, and more - and talk about them before diving into an hour of silent reading. This movement is happening all throughout the country and has really picked up traction over the last few years; I've been going to mine since 2023. Plus, there are about a dozen different Silent Book Clubs in Pittsburgh, so you can go to the one closest to you.
Carnegie Library Book Clubs | Various
If you’ve never participated in a book club, you’re really missing out! Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh is proud to offer an extensive array of book clubs to our community. In libraries throughout the city, you can find vibrant clusters of readers getting together to chat about books.
Pages & Pours Book Club | Various
Welcome to Pages & Pour Book Club Pittsburgh! BookTok is wild and it feels like everyone is always looking for a book club to join, hence why this book club was created in 2023! This club meets throughout the city at various different restaurants and bars, so keep an eye on the club's Instagram for that month's book and location.
Page Turners Book Club | Bakery Square
Page Turners Book Club is a free, women's community book club. They meet once a month to talk books, try a new craft, support local businesses, and make new friends!
Coffee Shops to Get Your Day Started
Commonplace Coffee | 1501 Buena Vista Street (North Side)
Commonplace Coffee has several locations throughout the city, but the location in the North Side's Mexican War Streets is this author's personal favorite. With a rustic vibe, Mason jars for cups, and pastries that will make you want seconds, this corner coffee shop is like a treat at the end of your busy day or the perfect beginning to an adventure.
La Prima Espresso Company | 205 21st Street (Strip District) & 1100 Smallman Street (Downtown)
La Prima is a go-to for those craving an authentic coffee taste but have a bit of a sweet tooth. If you plan to hang out for a while, head to the Downtown location; otherwise, we cannot recommend the Strip District spot enough. This counter-service shop is always packed, but we promise the wait is worth the taste. Plus, this location is attached to a restaurant that serves Italian baked goods, paninis, and cafe-style foods, so it really is your one-stop shop for a coffee and quick bites.
Allegheny Coffee and Tea Exchange | 2005 Penn Avenue (Strip District)
Located in the heart of Pittsburgh's historic Strip District, Allegheny Coffee & Tea Exchange is your location for some of the best coffee in the ‘burgh.
Delanie's Coffee | 1737 E Carson Street (South Side)
Looking for great coffee on Carson St? Delanie's Coffee SouthSide offers handcrafted espresso drinks, delicious breakfast options, and a cozy neighborhood cafe.
De Fer Coffee & Tea | 2002 Smallman Street (Strip District)
De Fer Coffee & Tea is an employee owned and operated specialty coffee company with five cafes in Pittsburgh, but the Strip District location is this author's favorite. A loft-style observation area is the perfect spot for you to read and people watch all day.
Afters Cafe | 1931 Murray Avenue (Squirrel Hill)
The cozy cafe serving espresso all day long! Afters Cafe is more than just a coffee shop - it's your one-stop shop for late night caffeine and a cozy place to hunker down with a book for the day.
Parks That Are Perfect to Read In
Schenley Park | Oakland
Created in 1889 with land donated by heiress Mary Schenley the park now contains 456 acres of trails, woods, and attractions. Grab your picnic gear and your book and catch the sunset on Flagstaff Hill.
Allegheny Commons Park | North Side
Allegheny Commons has anchored the North Side for over 150 years. The public commons predate the City of Pittsburgh's annexation of Allegheny City. This treasured Victorian gem is our oldest park and recalls the grandeur of Central Park.
Mellon Park | Shadyside
The Mellon Park Walled Garden allows you to step back into the gilded age. The Garden was part of the original Mellon Estate and served as their "back yard". The plantings - many of which are original hardscapes - feature flowering trees, shrubs, and perennials. The Garden's Japanese Stewarita tree is not native to our area.
Frick Park | Point Breeze
Frick Park is an ideal escape from the noise of the city. Known as Pittsburgh’s woodland park for its extensive trails throughout steep valleys and wooded slopes.
Point State Park | Downtown
Point State Park sits at the confluence of Pittsburgh's Three Rivers. The triangular plot of land at the tip of "The Golden Triangle" is a significant place Pittsburgh's story and the birthplace of our community. It is a gathering place for Pittsburgh's residents, the County, and the Commonwealth for grand civic events, and is a point of pride in our region.
Fill Your Days with Food
Breakfast
The Speckled Egg | 428 S. 27th Street (South Side)
A neighborhood diner whipping up omelets, sandwiches, cocktails and espresso in a friendly atmosphere.
Pamela's Diner | 60 21st Street (Strip District)
A unique, independently owned breakfast/lunch restaurants that was voted best breakfast in Pittsburgh and named in the top 10 of USA's Hotcakes. There are several locations, including in Millvale, Mt. Lebanon, Oakland, and Shadyside, but the original location in the Strip is a must-visit.
Pigeon Bagels | 5613 Hobart Street (Squirrel Hill)
Pigeon Bagels is a kosher bakery in Pittsburgh serving hand-rolled bagels, pastries and coffee.
Lunch
The Porch at Schenley | 221 Schenley Drive (Oakland)
The Porch features thoughtfully-crafted seasonal food made with fresh ingredients from local farms, coupled with the best outdoor dining in Pittsburgh. It's also a quick walk away from the Carnegie Library's main campus.
Ritual House | 524 William Penn Place (Downtown)
Spirited food & drink in an atmosphere designed for the senses. Ritual House was inspired by world travels and a passion for Pittsburgh. You're invited to make Ritual House your sanctuary and create your own rituals whether that's toasting major moments or gathering with people you love.
The Abbey on Butler Street | 4635 Butler Street (Lawrenceville)
The Abbey on Butler Street gives a sense of place and Pittsburgh history, the feeling that you've discovered something novel, yet close to home. They've brought together a coffeehouse, two bars, and a dining venue all under a single roof.
Dinner
Scratch & Co. | 1720 Lowrie Street (Troy Hill)
Located in the heart of Troy Hill, Scratch & Co. is more than just a restaurant; it’s a vital part of the Pittsburgh community. Here, connection, comfort, and support thrive, creating a welcoming atmosphere for friends to gather, laugh, and share memorable meals.
Novo Asian Food Hall | 1931 Smallman Street (Strip District)
Located inside The Terminal, Novo is a locally owned food hall serving fresh, authentic Asian dishes from multiple vendors with a lively bar and communal dining.
Fig & Ash | 514 E Ohio Street (Allegheny Center)
Fig & Ash blends rustic charm with upscale dining in Pittsburgh's North Side. Wood-fired dishes, craft cocktails, and seasonal ingredients set us apart.
Over Eden | 177 40th Street (Lawrenceville)
Located inside TRYP by Wyndham Pittsburgh/Lawrenceville Boutique Hotel. High above Eden Way, nestled in the rooftop of a former trade school in Pittsburgh’s Lawrenceville neighborhood, is a restaurant and garden bar waiting to be discovered. Enter Over Eden, where cultures intersect, glasses are raised, and plates are shared…And there’s a view.
Drinks
The Library on Carson | 2302 E Carson Street (South Side)
The Library on Carson brings a distinctive blend of classic bar culture and social energy that stands out along Pittsburgh’s vibrant Carson Street corridor. It attracts guests who enjoy spaces with character — places that feel storied and welcoming rather than generic. From the moment you walk in, the venue strikes a balance between warmth and excitement, making it easy to linger with friends and enjoy the night at your own pace.
Storyville Lounge | 753 E Warrington Ave (Allentown)
Inspired by Spirits is conjuring New Orleans’ infamous turn-of-the-century red light district. Located inside Dr. Tumblety's Time-Inspired Specialty Shop, this speakeasy blends vintage with classic.
Margaux | 5947 Penn Avenue (East Liberty)
Inspired by the idea of hybrid European café-bar, Margaux offers specialty coffee, shareable plates, rotating cocktails, a handpicked wine and beer list.
Commerce Bar | 128 S Highland Avenue (East Liberty)
A hidden cocktail bar in East Liberty that provides an elegant-yet-underground feel that feels like stepping into another world.
Stay & Transportation
Where to Stay
If you're coming for a day, you'll want to make it two. If you're coming for longer, you'll want to make sure you're staying at the right place for you. Luckily, Pittsburgh is filled with world-class hotels and resorts that can act as your home base, whether right in the heart of the action or outside of the bustling downtown areas.
Getting Around
Finding your way around a new place can be stressful, but it won't be here. Take the Duquesne Incline or Monongahela Incline up and down Mt. Washington for breathtaking views of the city and a journey to the shops on Shiloh Street, hop on a Pittsburgh Regional Transit bus for cheap, or take the "T" light rail system - which is free between Downtown and the North Side - for a hassle-free adventure.
