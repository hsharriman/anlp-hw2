Pittsburgh Veterans Day Parade
Celebrate those who have served and honor the men and women who fought for the country this Veterans Day with the Pittsburgh Veterans Day Parade.
Event Details
What to Expect
History
FAQ
We proudly celebrate the efforts of those who have served and commemorate those who lost their lives for our country with an annual parade. Many veterans call Pennsylvania home. The state is a hub for veterans, hosting the 4th largest population of men and women who served the country. With this title, it's no surprise that the oldest and largest Veterans Day Parade can be found right here in PA, in the heart of downtown Pittsburgh.
Event Details
When: Saturday, November 7, 2026 | 10:30 am
Where: Starts at the intersection of 11th Street and Liberty Avenue, then proceeds down Grant Street and the Boulevard of the Allies.
**This is a rain-or-shine event, so pack your umbrella if the weather requires it.
What to Expect
The Parade
Our Veterans Day parade will have a big lineup of featured guests and floats. Keep an eye out for military units, vintage vehicles, community groups, youth groups, and local businesses. Among these organizations, be sure to show thanks to the men and women who come from all eras and have fought and served to protect the country, who will also be walking in the parade.
VIP Experience
When: Thursday, November 5, 2026 | 6 pm to 10 pm
Where: Le Mont Restaurant | 1114 Grandview Avenue
Tickets: $150
Before the parade, a VIP dinner will be hosted at Le Mont Restaurant in Mount Washington to celebrate and connect veterans. Purchasing a ticket allows you to enjoy a gourmet meal while listening to stories and tributes.
This evening is meant to spotlight veterans who served between the years of 1975 and 2001. If you are a veteran who served within this time frame and are interested in attending, please contact Nathan Blouse at nathan.blouse.pvdpc@gmail.com to get registered for the experience.
**Please note that formal attire is suggested for this experience; however, it is not required.
History of Veterans Day in the United States
Before the Treaty of Versailles was signed on June 28, 1919, marking the end of World War I, an armistice was agreed upon on November 11, 1918. The armistice — an agreement between two warring parties to cease hostilities — was the first sign of the end of WWI.
In November 1919, President Woodrow Wilson announced November 11 as Armistice Day. He declared that the day should be used to honor those who had fought and served in the Great War. Despite this official declaration in 1919, it took until 1938 for the day to be honored as a national holiday. At the time, this day was meant to specifically honor World War I veterans.
By 1954, the number of American veterans had increased substantially due to those who served in WWII. To show respect and commemorate the soldiers, Armistice Day was changed to Veterans Day so that all military personnel from both WWI and WWII could be honored.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can I volunteer at the Veterans Day Parade in Pittsburgh?
Yes, you’re able to volunteer at the parade! Contact the Veterans Day Parade organization via email for more information. Another great way to help out with the parade is by donating!
The Veterans Day Parade Fund is always looking for donations so it can continue hosting the parade year after year. Or consider sponsoring a table for veterans in attendance at the VIP Experience.
Veterans Day Parade Fund
VIP Experience Sponsorship
When was the first Pittsburgh Veterans Day parade?
The first Pittsburgh Veterans Day Parade was in 1919, following the end of World War I.
What is parking like for the parade?
There are plenty of public parking garages where you can park your car and walk to the event.
Some nearby parking garages:
Mellon Square Garage
Oliver Garage
Smithfield Liberty Garage
Chatham Parking Garage
Can I use public transportation to get to the parade?
Yes! Given the parade’s procession in the city, it may be best to consider taking our public transit to attend the parade. Visit our Public Transportation page for more information on available options.
Are there any war memorials or veteran memorials I can visit in Pittsburgh?
Yes! Below is a list of our public war memorials. Be sure to pay tribute and stop by these locations on Veterans Day.
Korean War Memorial
Southwestern Pennsylvania World War II Memorial
Soldiers & Sailors Hall and Museum
Pittsburgh Allegheny County Vietnam Veterans Monument
What are the resources available to Pittsburgh veterans?
The best places to find available resources are the Pittsburgh Veterans Center (2500 Baldwick Rd #15) and Veterans Place (945 Washington Blvd). These locations have information about support groups, mental health support, medical support, mentorship programs, job training, and more.
