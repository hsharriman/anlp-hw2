Regent Square
A Tree-Lined Neighborhood Defined by Small-Town Charm
Tucked along the eastern edge of Frick Park, Regent Square is one of Pittsburgh’s most walkable neighborhood gems, with leafy streets, independent shops, and an easygoing village feel. Around South Braddock and Trenton Avenues, you’ll find the kind of local spots residents love to share: cozy cafés, curated bookstores, neighborhood pubs, handcrafted goods, and quick access to some of the city’s best wooded trails. Come for a hike, stay for a slow stroll down historic brick streets, and you’ll see why this East End neighborhood feels like a friendly escape just minutes from downtown Pittsburgh.
5 Ways to Explore Regent Square
1. Step Directly from Main Street to Forest Trails
Few places in the city let you transition so seamlessly from a boutique coffee shop to deep forest trails. As a primary gateway to the 644-acre Frick Park, Regent Square lets you start your morning with a stroll along Nine Mile Run or a brisk hike under lush forest canopies, all just steps from South Braddock Avenue.
2. Experience an Independent, Neighbor-Driven Dining Scene
Regent Square’s culinary offerings are proudly local and packed with character. Grab a casual bite and pick out a craft brew from the famous "Beer Cave" at D’s Six Pax & Dogz, or gather for dinner and creative cocktails in the Pacific Northwest-inspired dining room at Hemlock House. From cozy Italian bistros to neighborhood taverns, every spot feels like a local secret.
3. Stroll & Shop South Braddock and Trenton Avenues
The tree-lined corridors along South Braddock and Trenton Avenues host an eclectic collection of independent shops. You can pick up fresh sourdough croissants at Madeline’s Bakery, grab an espresso at Biddle's Escape, discover your next read at Stay Gold Books, or browse handcrafted home goods at Workshop PGH. Outdoor enthusiasts can also drop into 3 Rivers Outdoor Co. for gear, apparel, and community events.
4. Discover Historic Streetscapes & Architecture
Take a self-guided walking tour down the neighborhood’s historic brick-paved side streets. The quiet, shaded blocks are lined with beautifully preserved early 20th-century American Foursquares, Craftsman bungalows, and welcoming front porches that showcase quintessential East End neighborhood character.
5. Experience Iconic Community Festivals
Plan your trip around one of the neighborhood’s annual gatherings. Experience the excitement of the Run Around the Square (RATS) 5K and dog walk every August, hunt for vintage treasures at the multi-block Annual Yard Sale in the spring, or enjoy live music and local food vendors during the autumn Fall Festival.
