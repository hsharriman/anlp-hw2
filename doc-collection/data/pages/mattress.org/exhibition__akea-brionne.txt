Our Mission
Mattress Factory is an artist-centered museum, international residency program and renowned producer and presenter of installation art. We say “yes” to artists, offering time and space to dream and realize projects in our hometown, Pittsburgh, PA. We invite audiences from around the world and around the corner to step inside, immerse and connect with the artistic process.

Home Is Not A Place
This play is a reflection on the (im)possibility of accepting diversity and the other. The fragmented body of the neoplasm—the fruit of unstable conditions—overcomes barriers, loves and denies itself and others, wanders around, forgetting its profession. It frequently and with pleasure divides, goes through dangerous palpation, questions the possibility of contact with the experience of the other. Poorly brought up but very successful, it invites us to a trans-species transition.

Home Is Not a Place
Materials: Muslin, cotton, glitter, tile, grout, wood, thread, fabric adhesive, and velcro.
I was eight years old when Hurricane Katrina hit New Orleans. It was my first true understanding that the concept of home could disappear. As Louisiana continues to lose land, I find myself reckoning with the possibility that my home could one day become a myth, like Atlantis.
What unsettles me about Atlantis is that we no longer know whether it was ever there. Its existence is contested; a place has become a story, passed down through generations, suspended somewhere between history and myth. I wonder what it would mean for the place I come from to be known only by future generations through photographs, objects, recipes, music, smells, oral histories, and the memories of people who can say, 'I was there.'
A row house is not necessarily a home. A row house is solid and fixed. A brick and wood building sharing common wall, roofline and exterior design with adjacent neighborhood structures. The façade of any building is that threshold where we step from public exposure toward private inclusion. A home is feeling and relations. A place of sentiment and memory, sharing patterns of inheritance and refuge.
What row house and home share in common is lack of permanence. Buildings and neighborhoods are torn down and replaced. While feelings of home and belonging can travel with you through smell, touch, or remembrance as buildings come and go, so too can the sense of home be abruptly wrenched away by act of nature or man. A row house’s ability to become a home must be actively made and protected in relation to world and others. Brionne is concerned with the ways we know, show, and more importantly feel this care.
For Brionne, this textile row home emphasizes fabric’s natural inclination to define through flexibility, portability, and permeability, claiming softness and reflection as bodily responses more binding than claims to property or permanence.
Akea Brionne born 1996, New Orleans, LA; lives and works Detroit, MI.

About The Artist
Akea Brionne (b. 1996, New Orleans, LA, USA) is an interdisciplinary researcher and artist, working at the intersection of lens and fiber based media. Her practice explores the relationship between colonial and imperialist history and their impact on identity politics, cultural storytelling, memory, and assimilation. Her work analyzes this primarily through the observation within the African Diaspora, with a particular emphasis on Creole culture.
