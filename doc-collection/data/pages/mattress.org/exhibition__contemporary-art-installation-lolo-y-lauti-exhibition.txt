Our Mission
Mattress Factory is an artist-centered museum, international residency program and renowned producer and presenter of installation art. We say “yes” to artists, offering time and space to dream and realize projects in our hometown, Pittsburgh, PA. We invite audiences from around the world and around the corner to step inside, immerse and connect with the artistic process.

Triple Threat
This play is a reflection on the (im)possibility of accepting diversity and the other. The fragmented body of the neoplasm—the fruit of unstable conditions—overcomes barriers, loves and denies itself and others, wanders around, forgetting its profession. It frequently and with pleasure divides, goes through dangerous palpation, questions the possibility of contact with the experience of the other. Poorly brought up but very successful, it invites us to a trans-species transition.

Three channel video (6:59 min.), LED screens, mirrors, wooden ballet barre with metal brackets
Triple Threat  looks reassuringly familiar: a dance studio. A ballet barre runs along the wall, facing a continuous mirror. The proportions feel instructional and preparatory—a room for practice, correction, and becoming better versions of oneself. Yet this familiar setup does not quite settle. Three screens line the barre, introducing another visual register. Across the synchronized screens and their echoes, three dancers move in silence through choreographic sequences drawn from classic Broadway and Hollywood musicals: Sweet Charity, The Pajama Game, and Promises, Promises.
Shaped by stage director/choreographers such as Bob Fosse and Michael Bennett, these works belong to a moment when musical theater and television dance troupes delivered stylized bodies into American living rooms with clockwork precision. Legs snap, hips angle, gestures sharpen. For many queer viewers, these dancers were more than entertainment. They were queer “identificators”—early sites of recognition —as Lolo & Lauti describe them. They offered clues and codes—lessons in how to move, how to feel, how to be seen. A social choreography learned from screens, perfected in private, and performed as if survival depended on it.
If the musical references gesture toward [the stage or the performance or the limelight] as a site of polish and pleasure, this installation stretches that allure rather than dispelling it. The studio mirror catches and multiplies the images from the screens, folding live space and mediated space into one another. The studio becomes less a site of self-mastery than a chamber of circulation. Reflection and projection coexist; self-recognition and spectacle overlap, where training opens onto ritual and repetition becomes transformative. The absence of sound sharpens this multiplicity. Movement unfolds without its musical anchor, bringing forward labor: rehearsal, repetition, effort. Pleasure and discipline blur. Desire reveals itself as a force that carries repetition beyond the pleasure principle, one that draws the subject beyond comfort toward exposure and transformation.
In show business, a triple threat can sing, dance, and act. The phrase holds both promise and pressure. It suggests excess—a body asked to do more, stay visible, keep going. Thus, queerness emerges not as a refuge from risk, but as an invitation to vulnerability - a call to move between registers and inhabit shine and shadow at once, Like the musical, like the coven, like the stage itself, this is a space where transformation happens through aspiration, repetition and exposure. In Triple Threat, as in the queer aesthetic lineages Lolo & Lauti claim, where there is danger there is also salvation.
Text by Mariano López Seoane
Lolo y Lauti (Buenos Aires, 1980 y 1986) es un dúo de artistas que trabajan juntos desde el 2011. Su obra incluye performance, video, escultura, instalaciones y fotografía presentadas en formatos que van desde la exhibición a la ópera, pasando por las redes sociales y la realidad virtual. Lolo y Lauti reivindican el humor como experiencia autónoma y herramienta para abordar temas como la sexualidad, las drogas, la muerte y el arte. Su iconografía proviene del mundo del entretenimiento de internet y la televisión, pero también de las revistas y los parques de diversiones. Personajes de ficción, como Mafalda y divas de la TV como Moria Casán se encuentran bajo el mismo spotlight en un remix lisérgico plagado de reminiscencias teatrales.
Así como incluyen elementos de los mass media en el arte, piensan el arte con estrategias de los mass media. No ponderan ni denostan sus referencias, sino que las hipertrofian hasta el punto en que se funden la crítica y el fanatismo. La principal operación del dúo es la apropiación, una estrategia compartida por el arte contemporáneo y el espectáculo argentino, que funciona, muchas veces, como apropiador del espectáculo internacional. Y así como Susana Gimenez se apropia de Broadway, Lolo y Lauti se apropian de Susana, en un señalamiento en clave queer del libre juego de los símbolos de la cultura popular.

About The Artist
Lolo y Lauti is a duo of contemporary Argentine artists who have been working together since 2011. Their work includes video, performance, sculpture, installations and photography presented in formats ranging from exhibition to opera, through social networks and virtual reality. They claim humor as an autonomous experience and a tool to address topics such as sexuality, drugs, death and fine arts. They have shown their work in museums, galleries and festivals in Germany, Austria, Belgium, Chile, Colombia, Spain, United States, Greece, Italy, Mexico, Panama, Peru, United Kingdom and Uruguay. They held residencies at Villa Lena and SEA in Greece, and at ISCP in New York, among others. They recently obtained the 2022 Buenos Aires Art Week Award and the 2023 Amigos del Bellas Artes + ArtHaus Performing Arts Prize, as well as the Williams Foundation and Argentine Mozarteum Research Scholarship at the Cité Internationale des Arts in Paris. They also have a career as curators, having organized PERFUCH (Argentina’s first performance arts festival) in all its editions, as well as curating many individual and group shows in Buenos Aires.
