Our Mission
Mattress Factory is an artist-centered museum, international residency program and renowned producer and presenter of installation art. We say “yes” to artists, offering time and space to dream and realize projects in our hometown, Pittsburgh, PA. We invite audiences from around the world and around the corner to step inside, immerse and connect with the artistic process.

This play is a reflection on the (im)possibility of accepting diversity and the other. The fragmented body of the neoplasm—the fruit of unstable conditions—overcomes barriers, loves and denies itself and others, wanders around, forgetting its profession. It frequently and with pleasure divides, goes through dangerous palpation, questions the possibility of contact with the experience of the other. Poorly brought up but very successful, it invites us to a trans-species transition.

Untitled Second Floor
(ONE BIG DARK ROOM)
Materials: Sheetrock, 2 X 4s, window and door trim, MDF, recycled wood, plywood, 3D printed resin, latex paint, acrylic paint, watercolor, gilding wax, charcoal, canvas dropcloth, carpet, digital prints, architectural prints, laser prints, painter's tape, graphite, sharpie, ink, flood-damaged silver gelatin prints, acrylic shadowboxes, folding tables, pre-owned objects, materials, and fabrics sourced from the online marketplace.
For Amanda Ross-Ho, memory, hyperbole, duration, and myth-making are tools we use to register both loss and comprehension. Untitled Second Floor (ONE BIG DARK ROOM) is a 75% scale model replicating the second floor of Ross-Ho's Chicago childhood home. Scaled to fit the gallery, this installation forms the most recent in an ongoing body of work using archival research as experimental material and conceptual framework.
In the late ‘70s-early ‘80s the artist's mother, Laurel M. Ross, an artist and photographer, produced a series of photographs looking inward at the densely adorned rooms of their home as subject. In the photographs, private spaces of the home, the parents' bedroom, Ross-Ho’s bedroom, and the family darkroom, are remade into sites of artistic staging and aesthetic study. A set of negatives and prints from this domestic series suffered flood exposure in the house, sealing them together for nearly four decades. With the help of a conservator, the materials were separated but sustained significant damage.
Treating these images like continuity shots used for film production, Ross-Ho replicates the home as a set, aided by her own recollection and architectural details sourced from an online Zillow listing. The impossibility of stable translation between these three corrupted and incomplete sources turns the surrogate home into an apparatus for staging new gestures and images.
Untitled Second Floor (ONE BIG DARK ROOM) is a site for corporeal and emotional analysis. Specifics are forgotten, slights swell into catastrophes, effort extends until recognition, the everyday is remade as legendary—tuning the connective ties between the personal and the collective to an uncanny level. From within this home, Amanda Ross-Ho invites us to navigate the ongoing farce of developing a sense of continuity from sets of misremembered, reenacted, and excised fragments.

About The Artist
Amanda Ross-Ho was born in Chicago in 1975. She currently lives and works in Los Angeles. Ross-Ho has exhibited widely in museums and galleries worldwide, including solo exhibitions at Kunsthall Stavanger, Stavanger, Norway (2019) Bonner Kunstverein, Bonn (2017); Vleeshal, Middleburg (2016); Praz-Delavallade, Paris (2015); The Museum of Contemporary Art, Cleveland (2014); and the Museum of Contemporary Art, Los Angeles (2012). Her work has been included in group exhibitions at The Walker Art Center, Minneapolis (2016), Orange County Museum of Art, Newport Beach (2011); Henry Art Gallery, Seattle (2010); the Museum of Modern Art, New York (2010); the Whitney Biennial, New York (2008); among many other institutions.
