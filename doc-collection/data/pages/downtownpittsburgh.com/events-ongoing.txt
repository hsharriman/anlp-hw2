Public Utility Commission: runs Friday, October 30, 2026 through Sunday, March 7, 2027, The Goddard School of Pittsburgh - Downtown, 812 Liberty Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15222
Organized by Pittsburgh Cultural Trust.
A public utility commission is the regulatory body that oversees providers of essential services, such as water, gas, electricity, rail, and transportation, among others. In practice, corporate capture has often led to these bodies running cover for private corporations with monopolistic reach. How they behave in the dawning era of data center buildouts is a shadow looming on the horizon. If we reconsidered ‘public utility’ in the broadest manner, what might that phrase suggest? What would it look like if there were such a commission dedicated to all the little problems and solutions of making life more functional, and fruitful? And what if governance were so shaped that it was our working partner in that realization of our shared reality, down to those minute details? The exhibition “Public Utility Commission” is an attempt to dial that imagination into some clarity. As such, it is a reflection on privatization and fundamental issues of human spirit—like what we owe one another, and the shared responsibilities to those we exist beside—while predatory forces do their best to pollute and diminish the spaces we tenuously hold together. “Public Utility Commission” features the work of a robust roster of artists—internationally, from England, France, Italy, and the Republic of Georgia; regionally, from Lancaster, Morgantown, Philadelphia, and New York City; and many, locally, from Pittsburgh. “Public Utility Commission” is designed and curated by Jacquet Kehm and presented by the Pittsburgh Cultural Trust. Charlie Wallace: The Stars Are Watching: runs Friday, October 30, 2026 through Sunday, April 11, 2027, Sign of Light, 630 Stanwix St, Pittsburgh, PA 15222
Organized by Pittsburgh Cultural Trust.
The Pittsburgh Cultural Trust is pleased to present its fourth exhibition in collaboration with VaultArt Studios, a Pittsburgh-based non-profit and project of Achieva dedicated to providing a professional studio environment for artists with disabilities. This ongoing partnership reflects a sustained institutional commitment to expanding the field of contemporary art by ensuring that artists with disabilities are not positioned at its margins, but are recognized as vital contributors to its present and future. This exhibition features a new body of work by Charlie Wallace, a founding member of VaultArt Studio, whose practice engages deeply with the visual language of celebrity culture and is distinguished by his large-scale, meticulously rendered paper doll compositions. For this presentation, Wallace turns his attention to the Major Arcana of the tarot—a sequence of 22 cards that have, for centuries, functioned as a symbolic system for mapping human experience, transformation, and fate. Across art history, tarot imagery has served as a rich site of interpretation, from its origins in Renaissance Europe to its reimagining by modern and contemporary artists drawn to its dense layering of allegory, mysticism, and psychological insight. The Major Arcana in particular can be understood as a set of enduring archetypes—The Fool, The Magician, The Lovers, Death–figures that articulate universal narratives of beginnings, power, desire, loss, and renewal. Wallace reinterprets these archetypes through the lens of contemporary celebrity, translating each card into a carefully constructed likeness of a cultural icon. In doing so, he collapses distinctions between mythic and mediated identities, inviting viewers to consider how celebrity itself functions as a modern system of archetype—one through which collective aspirations, anxieties, and projections are continually performed and circulated. His signature two dimensional portrait format further underscores this dynamic, emphasizing themes of construction, projection, and transformation inherent to both tarot and celebrity culture alike. Charlie Wallace is guest curated by Steph Neary, VaultArt Studios in collaboration with the Pittsburgh Cultural Trust.

I Will Belong To Only Me: runs Sunday, November 22, 2026 through Friday, April 30, 2027, August Wilson African American Cultural Center, 980 Liberty Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15222, USA
Organized by AWAACC.
PEJU ALATISE: I WILL BELONG TO ONLY ME | weekdays 3pm-6pm, weekends 1pm-5pm Exhibition dates are available one month in advance with online sales closing 2 hours before the galleries open each day. Tickets can be purchased on site as a walk-in for same day or future dates. Titled after a line from her own writing, I Will Belong to Only Me marks the first U.S. solo survey of Peju Alatise (b. 1975 in Lagos, Nigeria). A defining figure in contemporary African art, this exhibition brings together two decades of her work across sculpture, installation, text, and film. Anchored by her celebrated installation Flying Girls (2015–16), I Will Belong to Only Me embodies Alatise’s artistic prowess, conceptual depth, and multidisciplinary command across architecture, painting, sculpture, and storytelling. Merging Yorùbá cosmology with poetic form, Alatise builds spaces where imagination is resistance and African girlhood is centered, protected, and celebrated. ABOUT THE ARTIST Peju Alatise (b. 1975, Lagos) is a Nigerian interdisciplinary artist, architect, and author whose practice spans sculpture, installation, painting, film, and fiction. She lives and works between Lagos, Nigeria and Glasgow, UK. Trained in architecture at Ladoke Akintola University of Technology, Alatise began her career balancing architectural work with studio practice. Her art engages themes of gender, power, spirituality, and African identity, often grounded in Yoruba cosmology and narrative traditions. In 2017, she was one of the artists selected for Nigeria’s historic debut at the 57th Venice Biennale, where her installation Flying Girls received international acclaim. That same year, she won the FNB Art Prize. Her work has since been exhibited globally, including at the Venice Architecture Biennale (2020), Frieze Sculpture (London, 2022), and is held in the collection of the Smithsonian National Museum of African Art, Chazen Museum of Art at University of Wisconsin, and the August Wilson African American Cultural Center.

Shikeith, Hold: runs Thursday, April 29, 2027 through Wednesday, June 30, 2027, Arts Landing, 8th St and Penn Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15222
Organized by Pittsburgh Cultural Trust.
Installed at Arts Landing, Hold is made of weathering steel and neon. The sculpture extends Project Blue Space, Shikeith’s initiative exploring the spiritual, cultural, and political relationships Black communities have forged with water. Its title gestures toward both the hold of a slave ship and the body as a vessel of memory, feeling, and endurance. Within, the work glows haint blue, a color rooted in Gullah Geechee traditions of protection. Its light pulses like breath. Over time, the steel exterior will deepen and shift with weather. About the Artist Shikeith is a visual artist and filmmaker whose work explores psychological landscapes and how history persists in the present. Hold, his first public art commission, brings this inquiry into civic space. Hold by Shikeith is commissioned by the Pittsburgh Cultural Trust for Arts Landing. Hold is developed as part of Project Blue Space, a web-based platform mapping the cultural, spiritual, and political relationships Black communities have forged with water. The project is funded by the Mellon Foundation’s Humanities in Place initiative.
