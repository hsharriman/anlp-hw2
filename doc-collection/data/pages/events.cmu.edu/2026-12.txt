The Posner Center is a cabinet of rarities and technological marvels: paper-and-string cipher machines, centuries-old books, cogwheel computers and 19th century “digital” images. Part museum and part laboratory, it is a site for discovery, research, and innovation with the materials of the past. Rare Books & Ancestral Machines features more than 30 books and objects from the Posner Center’s holdings. Many of these materials are on view for the first time. The exhibition inaugurates the newly renovated Center, surveying its collections and tracing more than four centuries of scientific inquiry and learning. Organized into five thematic sections — mechanical computing, robotics, cryptography, artificial intelligence, and scholarly method — the exhibit explores the histories of these disciplines, uncovering abandoned prototypes and long-forgotten technologies. CERCA Webinar with Orange Country Water District: Thursday, December 3, 2026, 3:30 PM - 4:30 PM, Virtual - Register Below
Hosted by Civil and Environmental Engineering Department.
“Co-creating tomorrow: Community Driven Engineering for a Resilient Future” is a webinar series exploring the critical intersection of community wisdom and engineering expertise in the face of a changing climate. Organized by CERCA, CMU CEE’s Center for Engineering Resilience and Climate Adaptation, this series will foster a deeper understanding of how community engagement can enhance the effectiveness, equity, and sustainability of climate adaptation initiatives. The Orange County Water District (OCWD) manages the Orange County Groundwater Basin, which provides approximately 85% of the water supply for 2.5 million people. Maintaining a reliable groundwater supply in a highly urbanized coastal basin requires an integrated approach that includes seawater intrusion protection, water reuse, stormwater capture, and managed aquifer recharge (MAR). Seawater intrusion has been a challenge in the Orange County Groundwater Basin since the 1920s. In response, OCWD constructed the Talbert Seawater Intrusion Barrier in the 1970s, creating a hydraulic barrier to protect the basin from further seawater intrusion. Today, OCWD continues to operate and improve its coastal barriers and is evaluating development of a new barrier in the Sunset Gap to address an additional potential pathway for seawater intrusion. Bill Leever, OCWD Principal Hydrogeologist, will discuss the history, operation, and future of OCWD’s seawater intrusion barrier program. Adam Hutchinson, OCWD Recharge Planning Manager, will discuss how OCWD uses Forecast-Informed Reservoir Operations (FIRO) and MAR to increase the capture and recharge of stormwater while maintaining flood-risk-management objectives. OCWD operates an extensive recharge system along the Santa Ana River and has worked with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for decades to temporarily store stormwater behind Prado Dam for subsequent release and groundwater recharge. Together, the presentations will illustrate how a coastal groundwater basin can integrate seawater intrusion protection, flood management, stormwater capture, groundwater recharge, and recycled water as complementary elements of a resilient regional water-supply system. Graduate Student Seminar: Friday, December 4, 2026, 12:30 PM, Wean Hall (Room 7500)
Hosted by Materials Science and Engineering Department.
“Harnessing Light, Color, Ions and Electrons: from Electrochromics to Artificial Retina,” presented by Jianguo Mei
Abstract: The Mei Research Group at Zhejiang University bridges fundamental discovery and real-world innovation by integrating chemistry, materials science, engineering and medicine. We develop organic semiconductors and conductors with transformative potential for printed electronics, bioelectronics, and living electronics, pursuing both technological impact and fundamental scientific frontiers. In this seminar, I will highlight two complementary research directions: Electrochromic Technologies and Living Electronics. Both are rooted in the precise control of light, color, ions, and electrons, yet extend these principles toward very different applications. Through Ambilight Inc., our discoveries in flexible electrochromic thin films have been translated into roll-to-roll manufacturing and commercialized for applications ranging from electric vehicles to consumer electronics. These examples illustrate how fundamental discoveries made in an academic laboratory can evolve into market-ready technologies. Our second direction, Living Electronics, asks a more fundamental question: Can electronics become part of living systems rather than being implanted into them? Inspired by biological vision, our work on Organic Eyes seeks to emulate the sensing and processing functions of the human visual system. In parallel, we are exploring electronics that can grow within the brain, creating electronic materials that form and function directly within living neural tissue. While these goals remain at the frontier of science, they point toward a fundamentally different relationship between synthetic materials and biology. To illustrate the broader philosophy behind our research, I will discuss the journey of n-doped poly(benzodifurandione) (n-PBDF)-from an unexpected polymer discovery and scalable synthesis to applications in flexible electronics and bioelectronics. This case study captures our approach: coupling curiosity-driven discovery with mission-driven innovation. By connecting fundamental chemistry to real-world needs while pursuing ambitious scientific frontiers, we seek to turn new molecular concepts into technologies that can ultimately benefit society. Kwanzaa Begins: Saturday, December 26, 2026 (all day)
Hosted by Inclusive Holiday Observations and Interfaith Calendar.
An African-American and Pan-African holiday celebrating family, community, and culture, Kwanzaa is a secular observance with some religious participation. Each day of Kwanzaa celebrates one of the Nguzo Saba, or the seven life principle in the language, Kiswahili. They are honored with candle-lighting each day of the season: umoja (unity), kujichagulia (self-determination), ujima (collective work and responsibility), ujamaa (cooperative economics), nia (purpose), kuumba (creativity), and imani (faith). Symbols include crops. Kwanzaa ends on January 1.

Watch Night or Freedom’s Eve: Thursday, December 31, 2026 (all day)
Hosted by Inclusive Holiday Observations and Interfaith Calendar.
Watch Night or Freedom’s Eve is celebrated in many African American or Black Christian Churches. It celebrates and commemorates the Emancipation Proclamation (enacted January 1, 1863) which freed American slaves during the American Civil War. Watch night worship services are now annual New Year’s Eve traditions, recalling faith, freedom, strength, and community. It is celebrated on the evening of December 31.
