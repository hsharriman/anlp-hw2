==Native American era== [[File:NativeTowns Pittsburgh.png|thumb|right|350px|Native Americans lived near the forks of the Ohio for thousands of years. These are some important villages, most c. 1750s, and earlier sites.]] For thousands of years, Native Americans inhabited the region where the Allegheny and the Monongahela join to form the Ohio. [[Paleo-Indian]]s conducted a [[hunter-gatherer]] lifestyle in the region perhaps as early as 19,000 years ago. [[Meadowcroft Rockshelter]], an [[archaeological site]] west of Pittsburgh, provides evidence that these first Americans lived in the region from that date.<ref name="NGRock">Shreeve, James. "The Greatest Journey," ''National Geographic'', March 2006, pg. 64.</ref> During the [[Adena culture]] that followed, [[Mound builder (people)|Mound Builders]] erected a large Indian Mound at the future site of [[McKees Rocks, Pennsylvania|McKees Rocks]], about three miles (5&nbsp;km) from the head of the Ohio. The Indian Mound, a burial site, was augmented in later years by members of the [[Hopewell culture]].<ref>{{cite news |first=Marylynne |last=Pitz |url =http://www.post-gazette.com/regionstate/20010513mound4.asp |archive-url =https://web.archive.org/web/20070513034839/http://www.post-gazette.com/regionstate/20010513mound4.asp |url-status =dead |archive-date =May 13, 2007 |title=Burial Mound to Get Historical Marker |newspaper=Pittsburgh Post-Gazette |date=May 12, 2001 |access-date=November 5, 2008}}</ref> By 1700 the [[Iroquois|Haudenosaunee]], the Five Nations-based south of the Great Lakes in present-day New York, held dominion over the upper Ohio valley, reserving it for hunting grounds. Other tribes included the [[Lenape]], who had been displaced from eastern Pennsylvania by European settlement, and the [[Shawnee]], who had migrated up from the south.<ref name="Sipe">Sipe, C. Hale, ''The Indian Wars of Pennsylvania'', 1831, Wennawoods Publishing reprint 1999</ref> With the arrival of European explorers, these tribes and others had been devastated by infectious diseases from Europe, such as [[smallpox]], [[measles]], [[influenza]], and [[malaria]], to which they had no immunity.<ref>Cook, Noble David. ''Born to Die: Disease and New World Conquest, 1492–1650,'' (1998)</ref> [[File:Darlington map of Pennsylvania 1680.png|thumb|left|250px|A map of Western Pennsylvania made in 1680 from the [[Darlington Collection]]]] In 1748, when [[Conrad Weiser]] visited [[Logstown]], {{convert|18|mi|km}} downriver from Pittsburgh, he counted 789 warriors gathered: the Iroquois included 163 [[Seneca nation|Seneca]], 74 [[Mohawk nation|Mohawk]], 35 [[Onondaga (tribe)|Onondaga]], 20 [[Cayuga nation|Cayuga]], and 15 [[Oneida tribe|Oneida]]. Other tribes were 165 [[Lenape]], 162 [[Shawnee]], 100 [[Wyandot people|Wyandot]], 40 [[Mississaugas|Tisagechroami]], and 15 [[Mahican|Mohican]].<ref name="Logs">Agnew, Daniel, Myers, Shinkle & Co., ''Logstown, on the Ohio'', 1894. pg. 7.</ref> [[Shannopin's Town]], a [[Lenape]] (Delaware) village on the east bank of the Allegheny, was established in the 1720s and was deserted after 1758. The village is believed to have been roughly from where [[Penn Avenue]] is today, below the mouth of Two Mile Run, from 30th Street to 39th Street. According to [[George Croghan]], the town was situated on the south bank of the Allegheny, nearly opposite what is now known as [[Herrs Island, Pittsburgh|Herr's Island]], in what is now the [[Lawrenceville (Pittsburgh)|Lawrenceville]] neighborhood in the city of Pittsburgh.<ref name = "Hanna1">[https://archive.org/details/wildernesstrailo01hann Charles Augustus Hanna, ''The Wilderness Trail: Or, The Ventures and Adventures of the Pennsylvania Traders on the Allegheny Path,'' Volume 1, Putnam's sons, 1911]</ref>{{rp|289}} [[Sawcunk]], on the mouth of the [[Beaver River (Pennsylvania)|Beaver River]], was a Lenape settlement and the principal residence of [[Shingas]], a chief of theirs.<ref>Buck, ''The Planting of Civilization in Western Pennsylvania,'' (1939), pg. 30.</ref> [[Chartiers Township, Washington County, Pennsylvania|Chartier's Town]] was a [[Shawnee]] town established in 1734 by [[Peter Chartier]]. [[Kittanning (village)|Kittanning]] was a Lenape and [[Shawnee]] village on the Allegheny, with an estimated 300–400 residents.<ref>''Course of Study in Geographic, Biographic and Historic Pittsburgh,'' The Board of Public Education, Pittsburgh, 1921.</ref>
