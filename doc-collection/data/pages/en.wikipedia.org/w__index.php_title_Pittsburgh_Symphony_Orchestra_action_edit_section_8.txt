===1995–2004: The second century=== A capital campaign was launched in 1993 to increase the Pittsburgh Symphony's endowment by $70&nbsp;million. On April 10, 1995, the orchestra announced the appointment of [[Mariss Jansons]] to succeed Maazel in 1996 as its eighth music director.<ref>{{cite news | url=http://www.post-gazette.com/pg/04137/315957.stm | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040621130655/http://www.post-gazette.com/pg/04137/315957.stm | url-status=dead | archive-date=June 21, 2004 | title=Fanfare for an uncommon man | work=Pittsburgh Post-Gazette | author=Andrew Druckenbrod | date=May 16, 2004 | access-date=2007-04-27}}</ref> Latvian–born Jansons was well received by critics and audiences in Pittsburgh, who applauded the "warmth and humanity" that he brought to the ensemble.<ref>{{cite news |first=Caroline |last=Abels |title=Sour Note |newspaper=[[Pittsburgh Post-Gazette]] |date=March 18, 2000 |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1129&dat=20000318&id=2l4xAAAAIBAJ&pg=6915,5652169}}</ref> With Jansons, the Pittsburgh Symphony toured worldwide and recorded extensively. During this time, the symphony was "innovative in drawing new elements of the Pittsburgh region’s population to concerts in [[Heinz Hall]] and elsewhere." Jansons conducted his tenure at the close of the 2003–2004 season.<ref>{{cite news |title=A maestro gives notice: Jansons and Pittsburgh made music together |newspaper=[[Pittsburgh Post-Gazette]] |date=June 10, 2002 |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1129&dat=20020610&id=xdJRAAAAIBAJ&pg=5739,8501583}}</ref>
