===Visual and Public Arts=== [[File:Wood Street Galleries - IMG 7572.JPG|thumb|A portion of Wood Street Galleries|left]] The Pittsburgh Cultural Trust features contemporary art exhibitions in the Wood Street Galleries, SPACE, 707 Penn, and 820 Liberty. The Trust also has seven [[public art]] projects on display year-round in Pittsburgh's [[Cultural District, Pittsburgh|Cultural District]]. They include the following: *'''[[Allegheny Riverfront Park]]''' located at the Allegheny riverfront, [[Rachel Carson Bridge]] to [[Fort Duquesne Bridge]]. The park was commissioned in the early 1990s when the PCT's District Plan included the creation of a riverfront park to border the northern boundary of the [[Cultural District, Pittsburgh]]. The PCT's then Public Arts Advisory Committee commissioned a first-time collaboration between artist [[Ann Hamilton (artist)|Ann Hamilton]] and landscape architect [[Michael Van Valkenburgh]] to create the park.<ref>{{cite web|title=Allegheny Riverfront Park|url=http://www.pps.org/great_public_spaces/one?public_place_id=603&type_id=1|publisher=Project for Public Spaces|access-date=8 July 2014|year=2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140704113610/http://www.pps.org/great_public_spaces/one?public_place_id=603&type_id=1|archive-date=4 July 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>[[File:Agnes R. Katz Plaza - IMG 7628.JPG|thumb|right|Agnes R. Katz Plaza at 7th Avenue and Penn Avenue]] * '''Agnes R. Katz Plaza''' located at 7th Avenue & Penn Avenue. Kata Plaza, commissioned in 1998, features [[Louise Bourgeois]] sculptures, including three granite benches shaped like eyeballs and the centerpiece 25-foot-tall bronze fountain. Landscape architect [[Dan Kiley]] and architect [[Michael Graves]] also worked on the project.<ref>{{cite web|title=Agnes R. Katz Plaza |url=https://tclf.org/sites/default/files/microsites/kiley-legacy/AgnesKatzPlaza.html| publisher=The Landscape Architecture Legacy of Dan Kiley |access-date=8 July 2014|year=2013}}</ref> * '''Haas Mural''' located on the Fort Duquesne Boulevard façade of the [[Byham Theater]], 101 6th Street. The 36-by-56-foot [[mural]] was commissioned in 1993 and painted by [[Richard Haas]] in the ''[[trompe-l'œil]]'' style.<ref>{{cite web|title=Haas Mural |url=http://pittsburghartplaces.org/accounts/view/Untitled_Fulton_Theater | publisher=Pittsburgh Art Places |access-date=8 July 2014|year=2012}}</ref> * '''Magnolias for Pittsburgh''' located at 7th Avenue & Penn Avenue. The Public Art Network of Americans for the Arts selected [[Tony Tasset]]'s "Magnolias for Pittsburgh" to be in the 2007 "Year in Review". The installation features two bronze [[magnolia]] trees, five live magnolia trees, and a landscape design.<ref name="magnolias">{{cite news | url = http://www.popcitymedia.com/innovationnews/magnolias0627.aspx | title = Magnolias for Pittsburgh wins national recognition | work = [[Pop City]] | date = June 27, 2007 | access-date = July 8, 2014 | last = Diamond Smit | first = Debra | archive-date = July 29, 2014 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20140729223328/http://www.popcitymedia.com/innovationnews/magnolias0627.aspx | url-status = dead }}</ref> * '''Momento Mori''' located at Tito Way & Exchange Way.<ref>{{cite web|title=Momento Mori| url= http://pittsburghartplaces.org/accounts/view/406 |work= Memento Mori | publisher= Pittsburgh Art Places | access-date=17 July 2014|year=2012}}</ref> * '''Sign of Light''' located at Penn Avenue Place, Stanwix Street & Fort Duquesne Boulevard, facing the [[Allegheny River]]. This 20-by-40-foot electronic sign is composed of [[LED]]s that project a white triangle floating on a blue-gray background. "Sign of Light" is visible from the [[North Side (Pittsburgh)]] and [[PNC Park]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Sign of Light| url= http://www.trustarts.org/visualarts/public_art/sign_of_light/ | publisher=Pittsburgh Cultural Trust | access-date=8 July 2014|year=2012–2014}}</ref>
