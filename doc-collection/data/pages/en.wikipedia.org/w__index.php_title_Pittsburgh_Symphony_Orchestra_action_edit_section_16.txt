===Other television relays=== The Pittsburgh Symphony under [[Gilbert Levine]] was joined by the London Philharmonic Choir, Kraków Philharmonic Choir, Ankara Polyphonic Choir, and members of the Mendelssohn Choir of Pittsburgh in a program that included the world premiere of "Abraham," a sacred motet by John Harbison, and Mahler Symphony No. 2 "Resurrection.<ref>{{cite news|last=Roddy|first=Dennis|title=Pope in Peace Concert|newspaper=Pittsburgh Post-Gazette|date=2004-01-08}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Pope Hosts Concert of Reconciliation |work=[[United Press International]] |url=http://www.upi.com/Pope-hosts-concert-of-reconciliation/22331074430452/ |date=January 18, 2004}}</ref> The event, which was attended by the Pontiff, Rav Elio Toaff, Imam Abdulawahab Hussein Gomaa, and 7,000 invited guests, was telecast on RAI, on PBS, and throughout the world, and released on DVD by WQED Multimedia, Pittsburgh. Subsequent to this concert, at Levine's impetus, the Pittsburgh Symphony founded a series of concerts entitled "Music for the Spirit."<ref>{{cite news |last=Kanny |first=Mark |title=Concert series aims to memorialize 'Music for the Spirit' |newspaper=[[Pittsburgh Tribune-Review]] |date=January 7, 2006 |url=http://triblive.com/x/pittsburghtrib/ae/music/s_411162.html}}</ref> He led the first two of these performances of the Haydn "Creation and Mahler Symphony No. 3 in Pittsburgh in 2006.<ref>{{cite news |last=Kanny |first=Mark |title=Gilbert, PSO showcase Mahler symphony |newspaper=[[Pittsburgh Tribune-Review]] |date=June 22, 2006 |url=http://triblive.com/x/pittsburghtrib/ae/more/s_458965.html}}</ref>
