In some countries, Gluck operas did not use trombones in his orchestras PreK

also a big instrument to move around. Look at some photos of a harp. What

Hector Berlioz was a French composer who decided to devote his life to music PreK
after hearing Gluck’s Orpheus & Eurydice. His music uses a large orchestra.
Listen to a short section of his Symphonie fantastique to identify some
orchestra instruments.
Gluck played a clavichord; it is pictured in his famous portrait. A clavichord is PreK
an instrument that looks like a piano but sounds different because of how the
strings are hit. Look at some photos of clavichords and pianos, and listen to
excerpts of each as they are played. Brainstorm a list of similarities and
differences that you observe.
Orpheus & Eurydice is an opera that tells a story from ancient Greek PreK
mythology. What kind of costumes should Orpheus and Eurydice be wearing

The chorus and dancers in Orpheus & Eurydice play nymphs, shepherds, ES

Lesson Plan Stems
There is a famous 1775 portrait of Gluck playing his clavichord, painted by ES
Joseph Duplessis. Before photographs were possible, how do we know what
famous people looked like? Would Gluck have had to “sit” for the painting?
Pittsburgh Opera will be producing and performing Orpheus & Eurydice for the ES
first time. Why do you think the company has never performed the opera

Gluck wrote operas that changed the style of and expectations for opera seria ES
and opera buffa. What does each term mean? What would you expect to
happen in an opera seria or an opera buffa? Which one—seria or buffa—does
Orpheus & Eurydice most closely resemble? Why?
In both the Italian and French versions, Orpheus's lyre is represented in the ES
orchestra by the harp, and it was this use of the instrument in 1774 that it is
thought to have introduced the harp to French orchestras. Is the harp used in
orchestras today? What is a lyre? How does it differ from a modern harp?
Depending on the number of strings on pedal harps (44 to 47 strings), lever ES
harps (34 to 38 strings), or lap harps (14 to 27 strings), how would a player
know which strings were which notes? Examine a harp. What do you notice
about the strings? Why might that be important for learning to play this
instrument? Does this remind you of any other stringed instrument? If so, why?
In the opera, Orpheus’s music charms the angry spirits who try to keep him ES
from entering the underworld. Consider the famous quote that posits music
has the power to soothe an angry soul. Do you believe that to be true? How is
music used to calm someone or something down? Can the opposite be true;
can music be used to call someone to action or anger someone or something?
Gluck’s work inspired Louis-Hector Berlioz (1803–1869,) who became a ES/MS
famous French Romantic composer, conductor, and critic. Berlioz’s work
includes orchestral compositions such as the Symphonie fantastique and
Harold in Italy, three operas (Benvenuto Cellini, Les Troyens, and Béatrice et
Bénédict), choral pieces, and works of hybrid genres such as the "dramatic
symphony" Roméo et Juliette and the "dramatic legend" La Damnation de
Faust. For which piece is Berlioz best remembered?
Orpheus & Eurydice is the most popular of Gluck's works and it influenced ES/MS
other German operas. Variations on its plot—the underground rescue mission
in which the hero must control, or conceal, his emotions—can be found in
Mozart's The Magic Flute, Beethoven's Fidelio, and Wagner's Das Rheingold.
What other musical works share these elements? What other musical works
have become an influence on other performance or narrative arts?
A modern orchestra harp has pedals that allow the harp to play chromatic ES/MS
notes over a range of six and a half octaves. Compare that range to the range
of a piano. Could a harp play piano music? Why or why not?

Gluck made several changes to the orchestration of Orpheus & Eurydice when MS
adapting it from his original Italian version to his French version. Cornetts and
chalumeaux were replaced by more common (and modern) oboes and
clarinets, while the trombone parts considerably decreased, possibly due to a
lack of technical ability on the part of the French trombonists. Is it surprising
that a composer would change a composition because of unskilled
instrumentalists? Why else might a composer change his composition? Do
modern composers adapt their work to the capabilities of the companies
performing it? Why or why not?
Gluck received a commission to write for the world-famous castrato Caffarelli. MS
What was a castrato? In what ranges did castratos, or castrati, sing? What
roles did they perform? The last castrato of the Sistine Chapel died in 1922.
Nowadays, who would sing the roles written for castrati? Using Operabase |
Opera Singers | Artists | Opera Companies | New & Rare Opera
Performances, research how many arias of Gluck’s time were written for

Greek myths, such as the stories of Orpheus and Eurydice, are a collection of MS
ancient folklore and religious stories that were originally spread through oral
traditions. To ancient civilizations, these timeless tales helped explain the
origins of the universe and the human condition. What is an oral tradition?
How is it performed? What human conditions are evident in the Orpheus
story?
Orpheus & Eurydice calls for three female singers, one of whom sings a male MS
role. Orpheus is written for a contralto or countertenor. The Euridice and
Amore roles are written for sopranos. Would the opera be considered a
chamber opera? How many operas have a small cast of three? How many
operas have only one leading role?
Gluck was revered by composers such as Mozart, Schubert, Berlioz, and MS/HS
Wagner for the radical reforms he brought to opera in the 1760s. What were
the changes Gluck made? What guiding principles or ideas led to these
changes? What other modern musicians or composers have also had a lasting
impact on their profession?

Lesson Plan Stems

Orpheus and Euridice has been adapted numerous times in multiple genres, MS/HS
including ballet, film and television, literature, music, song cycles, stage works,
video games, and visual arts. Why would an artist choose this ancient myth to
create works like Anaïs Mitchell’s Hadestown and Ricky Ian Gordon’s song
cycle?
Though originally set to an Italian libretto, Orphée et Eurydice owes much to MS/HS
French opera, particularly in its use of accompanied recitative and a general
absence of vocal virtuosity. Twelve years after the 1762 premiere, Gluck
adapted his opera to suit the tastes of a Parisian audience at the Académie
Royale de Musique, with a libretto by Pierre-Louis Moline. This reworking was
titled Orphée et Eurydice, and several alterations were made in vocal casting
and orchestration to suit French tastes. Which version will Pittsburgh Opera
use? How does an opera company decide which version to use?
The musical Hadestown by Anaïs Mitchell blends versions of two ancient MS/HS
Greek myths, Orpheus and Eurydice and Hades and Persephone, to explore
enduring and contemporary themes such as poverty. The musical has recently
been released for high school performances in the United States. How could
the musical be marketed to younger audiences?
Some significant elements of the musical Hadestown, which was partly MS/HS
inspired by Orpheus and Eurydice, include workers, movement, flurry dances,
and symbolism. In the musical, sometimes Hermes is cast as a man and
sometimes a woman. Explore and compare the musical Hadestown to Gluck’s
Orpheus Eurydice.
Orpheus & Eurydice is the first of Gluck's "reform" operas, in which he HS
attempted to replace the abstruse plots and overly complex music of opera
seria with a "noble simplicity" in both the music and the drama. Trace opera
history to highlight eras when the genre experienced profound “reforms.”
To supplement his earnings, Berlioz—who was inspired by Gluck—wrote HS
musical journalism throughout much of his career; some of it has been
preserved in book form, including his 1844 Treatise on Instrumentation, which
was influential in the 19th and 20th centuries. Who updated the book after its
initial publication? Is the book still in print?
Gluck was admired by other composers. His greatest French admirer was HS
Hector Berlioz, whose epic Les Troyens may be seen as the culmination of the
Gluckian tradition. Though Gluck wrote only one opera in German, his work
influenced the German school of opera, particularly composers Carl Maria von
Weber and Richard Wagner, whose concept of music drama was not so far
removed from Gluck's own. Create a “river of influence” graphic to
demonstrate how Gluck’s ideas flowed into other countries and eras.

In Gluck’s time, cornetts were instruments that were typically used for church HS

chalumeaux were to survive. Research other historical instruments that

Character Themes for Orpheus & Eurydice
Eleanore N. Childs, a Pittsburgh attorney and child advocate, worked with
teachers for more than a decade to research character education and develop the
Heartwood Ethics Curriculum for Children. Heartwood’s life lessons through
literature promote understanding and practice of seven universal character
attributes, which constitute the foundation of community among all people.
The Heartwood Institute defined seven universal character attributes:
 Courage is not only mastering fear; it is the ability to face difficulty or risk with integrity and
honor.
 Loyalty is being faithful to a person, group, or ideal based on understanding that we are all
part of something greater than ourselves.
 Justice is being guided by a balance of truth, reason, and fairness to all.
 Respect is regard for all life on earth. It calls us to value all people and their contributions,
viewpoints, and differences, such as culture, religion, age, race, and ability.
 Hope guides our future through faith and aspiration, helping us to rise above selfishness
and despair.
 Honesty is being truthful and honorable, the foundation for friendship and community.
 Love is deep care and concern for others, encompassing all the attributes. The more love
we give, the richer we become.
Discuss two attributes—Honesty and Love—after reading the synopsis of
Orpheus & Eurydice.
1. Trust is an outgrowth of honesty, as the ability to trust stems from the belief that
someone will be truthful and not break faith with another person. Based on her
remonstrances to Orpheus, does Eurydice trust him? What does he do that gives
her cause to doubt his honor and love? What does this tell us about both
characters, and perhaps their marriage?
2. In some versions of this myth, Orpheus is not considered an honorable man
because he does not commit suicide once Eurydice has been lost a second time,
and therefore his love cannot be true since he is unwilling to join her forever in the
Underworld. Do you think Orpheus is essentially an honest man? Why or why not?
3. In his effort to rescue Eurydice from the Underworld, is Orpheus attempting to
“cheat” death? Is that what an honest person would do—cheat? Should his love
for Eurydice override honorable behavior, or does his love reflect a different
aspect of honor? Eurydice is in Elysium, a kind of heavenly afterlife; is Orpheus’s
attempt to bring her back to the world an honest act of love, or is he acting more in
his own self-interest, since he cannot imagine life without her?
4. Amore sets the conditions for Orpheus to rescue Eurydice, and it is these
conditions that Orpheus feels forced to violate to assure Eurydice of his love.
Since Amore is the god of love, why does the god put conditions on Orpheus’s
journey? Is he testing Orpheus’s love? If so, why?
