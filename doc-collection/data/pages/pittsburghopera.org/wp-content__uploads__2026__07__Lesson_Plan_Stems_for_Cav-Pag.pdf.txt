Lesson Plan Stems in the English Language Arts and Mathematics
sections are Common Core Friendly, and are marked: CCF
Arts and Humanities
State Standards: 9.1, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4
At the end of an opera, all the performers appear together on stage for a PreK
“curtain call,” which is when the audience shows its appreciation for the
show. How do audiences show appreciation for performers? How do artists
thank audiences during a curtain call? At the premiere of Cavalleria
rusticana, its composer Pietro Mascagni took 40 curtain calls! What does
that tell you about this opera? How would you organize a curtain call for
either of these operas? Which characters go first? Last? Practice giving a
curtain call with your classmates.
Conductors lead an orchestra by showing them when and how to play the PreK
music together. How does a conductor show the musicians what tempo to
play? Imagine you are conducting an orchestra for an opera. How would you
keep the orchestra on tempo? Show how you might guide an orchestra to
change from one tempo to another.
Both operas use tubular bells in their orchestrations. Find a picture of tubular PreK
bells. How does a tubular bell look different from a bell you might see in a
bell tower? Where else might you have seen tubular bells?
For either opera, compare the traditional settings and costumes from the late ES
1800s with a production set in the present day. Why might a director choose
one type of setting and costume over another?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 2

Lesson Plan Stems
Story plots keep a reader interested by using complications, the twists and ES
turns that happen before a story is finished. Listen to a plot summary of
either opera. Count how many times something happens that creates a
change in the story. With a partner, draw a maze that reflects how many
twists and turns you counted for the story.
The orchestration for Pagliacci includes a bass tuba. What does it sound ES
like? What does it look like? Why did Leoncavallo score for this instrument,
rather than a Sousaphone? Could a Sousaphone be used in an orchestra
pit? Why did the composer write for a stage band (banda) and an orchestra
in the pit? What kinds of instruments appear in Cavalleria rusticana and
Pagliacci, but not in other operas from earlier eras?
Cavalleria rusticano and Pagliacci are usually performed together in one ES/MS
evening, in what is known as a “double bill.” Why are these two operas
double billed? What makes this pairing work? What other shows or movies
have also been “double billed”? Create your own double billing: what two
shows do you think should be shown together?
Pietro Mascagni and Ruggero Leoncavallo, the composers of Cavalleria ES/MS
rusticano and Pagliacci, are often considered to be “one-hit wonders”
because their other operas are seldom performed. What modern bands are
also considered “one-hit wonders”? Name some “one-hit wonder songs.”
Where do you hear this music played now? Can a performer make a living
from a one-hit wonder?
What are the musical characteristics of verismo, and how does this style ES/MS
apply to Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci? How are singers for a verismo
opera different from singers for a Mozart opera? How are verismo arias
different from arias by Mozart and arias by Verdi?
Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci are often performed as a double bill. ES/MS
Create a chart of pros/cons describing why Pagliacci should be performed
alone. When were the two operas first performed together? Why?
How does an opera company decide when to include an intermission at an ES/MS
opera performance? Pittsburgh Opera will include one intermission, between
the performances of Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci. What financial,
artistic, and other factors should be considered? Do any operas include
more than one intermission?
Giovanni "Gianni" Versace, a charismatic and accomplished Italian designer MS/HS
of clothing and theater costumes, was from Reggio Calabria, Italy, the same
location where the story of Pagliacci is set. How were Versace’s designs
influenced by Andy Warhol and modern abstract art?
Operas often contain small parts for characters that may be necessary for MS/HS
only a little piece of the story. How would you prepare if you were hired to
play a “bit part” in a professional production as opposed to a school
production? Debate the saying: “There are no small parts, only small actors.”
Pittsburgh Opera Education 3

Lesson Plan Stems
Cavalleria rusticana’s “Intermezzo” is the most famous piece of music from MS/HS
this opera. Listen to a recording of this piece. Where in the opera’s story is it
played? What does the music suggest? Why would Mascagni include this
music at this point in the story? What other movies or television shows have
used this music, and to what effect?
Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci are considered verismo operas. Based on MS/HS
the synopsis of both stories, what are the characteristics of verismo? The
first films screened in nickelodeons in the early 1890s, around the same time
as these operas premiered. What style of film seems most like these
verismo operas? What current TV shows could be described as verismo?
What features could you add to a theatrical production at a school to make it
verismo?
Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci are both through-composed operas. What MS/HS
does “through-composed” mean? What are the differences between a
through-composed opera and a number opera? How can listeners determine
when “scenes” begin and end? What other operas or musicals are also
considered through-composed?
The opera Pagliacci is named for the main characters, who comprise a MS/HS
traveling troupe of comedic entertainers. What kind of space would a small,
traveling troupe need for a performance? When might these performances
take place? Would the audience sit or stand? What would be the challenges
of having sound and light at night? How might technical factors like sound
and light affect audience etiquette?
Celebrities in the arts are sometimes placed on pedestals. What happens MS/HS
when the public sees their human faults? How does celebrity worship relate
to Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci?
The enduring “sad clown” image has become ingrained in popular culture MS/HS
largely due to Enrico Caruso’s wild success singing the role of Canio.
Caruso himself was a gifted caricaturist, and he often drew humorous
pictures of fellow performers. Look at some samples of Caruso’s drawings.
How does he convey humor in these? Create your own humorous
caricatures of friends, in the style of Caruso, imagining them as different
characters in Pagliacci.
Brainstorm the classical and modern meanings of comedy, and how they HS
have been used in the arts. Consider Pagliaccio’s statement that the
“comedy” is over. What are the many meanings of “comedy” in that context?
Create some theater improvisation situations based on scenes from HS
Cavalleria rusticana or Pagliacci. Create an activity like the comedy game
show “Whose line is it anyway?” What are the pros and cons of trying to be
creative in a competitive atmosphere?

Career Education and Work
State Standards: 13.1, 13.2, 13.3, 13.4
The title Pagliacci (or I Pagliacci) means “The Clowns.” What’s a clown? PreK
What do they look like? Where do we see them perform? What do they
do? Do you like clowns? Why or why not?
Mascagni’s Cavalleria rusticana was initially written for an opera PreK
competition, where three winners would have their operas performed in
Rome. What is a competition, and why do people enter them? Have you
ever participated in a competition? What type of prize would you compete
for?
Pietro Mascagni had a very successful career as both a conductor and a PreK/ES
composer. How are the professions of composing and conducting related
to one another? Do you know anyone who has more than one job?
Most operas are created through a collaboration of a composer and a ES/MS
librettist. What does it mean to collaborate? What other professions rely on
collaboration? What other professions—both on and off-stage—collaborate
to make an opera performance? Select one of those professions and
research what requirements are needed to work in that job.
What is a career like in the circus? Is it possible to have a career as a ES/MS
clown? How would one get training and work? What does a clown costume
represent? What does a clown’s makeup represent?
Both Mascagni and Leoncavallo became wealthy from the success of one
opera, despite each having written many more. How many performers
have built careers from “one-hit wonders”? How does early success impact
a performer’s career? Name some “one-hit wonders” that you know.
Leoncavallo, the composer of Pagliacci, was the son of a Neapolitan police MS/HS
magistrate. What authority does a magistrate have in both common and
civil law? Does this term for a judicial officer have the same meaning
worldwide? Can a lawyer become a magistrate?
Some famous performers began their careers as traveling street artists. MS/HS
What are the rules for legitimate street performers? How can working as a
street performer help build a career? How much does the average street
artist make? Can you support yourself this way? Brainstorm a list of artists
who began their careers “on the street.”
In Cavalleria rusticana, the character Turiddu is a returning soldier. What MS/HS
are the requirements for joining the army today? What are the pros and
cons of enlisting in the military? What are some of the career options
available for someone interested in a military career? What salary does an
entry level soldier make? What promotions are available, and how does an
enlisted person earn promotions? What percentage of high school students
choose to join the military? What is the average length of a military career?

Civics and Government
State Standards: 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4
In both operas, the main characters commit crimes in front of witnesses. PreK
What’s a witness? What does it mean to witness something?
In both Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci, one character kills another out PreK/ES
of jealousy. What is jealousy? What would you call the crimes these
characters have committed? How are criminals punished nowadays when
they commit crimes? Should these characters be punished, or are their
actions justifiable?
When both of these operas were written, Italy had a king. Does Italy still PreK/ES
have a king today? What form of government does Italy have now? Who is
the current head of the Italian government?
Both operas concern people seeking justice by taking the law into their ES/MS
own hands. What do Italians call their policemen? What do their uniforms
look like? What equipment do they carry? How do the duties and
expectations of Italian police compare to those of American police?
In both operas, murders are committed in front of a lot of witnesses, who ES/MS
do not intervene to prevent the crime. What are the responsibilities of
average people when they witness a crime? Is a witness morally or legally
obligated to help? Is there a difference between moral and legal
obligations? Can a citizen make an arrest?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 6

Lesson Plan Stems
Italians hold elections, as we do in the United States. Explore how Italian ES/MS
elections are structured. Who is eligible to vote? How is voting
accomplished? What political parties currently exist in Italy? How do those
parties compare to the political parties in the United States?
Explore the Italian court system and compare it to the judicial system in the ES/MS
United States. How is a typical trial in Italy conducted? How are judges
chosen; are they elected or appointed, and if so, by whom? Is there a jury?
If so, who may be part of a jury and how are they chosen? What rights do
defendants have?
The settings for these operas—Sicily and Calabria—are locations that MS/HS
historically have been plagued with organized crime. What is organized
crime? What types of organized crime exist in the United States? How do
these organizations compare to their Italian counterparts? How is
organized crime prosecuted in the United States? What statutes exist that
apply specifically to organized crime? How successful has United States
law enforcement been in prosecuting and convicting organized crime?
Consider this definition: “Murder is the unlawful killing of one human being MS/HS
by another through any action intended (or in some countries also being
recklessly indifferent) to kill or cause grievous bodily harm.” How does the
charge of murder vary between jurisdictions? In the United States, what
punishments can be meted out if a person is found guilty of murder? Are
these punishments the same regardless of location where a murder has
been committed? In the United States, if someone commits a serious crime
during which someone dies, can he/she be prosecuted for murder? What
are considered “serious crimes” in the United States?
Music from both Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci is often used in other MS/HS
popular culture entertainments, such as films and commercials. Who owns
the rights to this music, or can anyone use it for any purpose? Investigate
copyright laws. How are artists protected by these laws, and for how long?
Once an artist has died, do the copyrights also expire? How do opera
companies acquire permission to perform operas? Do any profits from
modern performances of older operas go back to the artists’ families or
their estates?
As with Taddeo the Hunchback in Pagliacci, characters with disabilities MS/HS
were often portrayed as humorous or evil, and thus become the recipients
of scorn and derision. Explore the Americans with Disabilities act, and the
laws that currently protect differently-abled people. How has the passage
of the ADA influenced how differently-abled people are treated in public
and in the workplace? Has this act also impacted their portrayal in art and
entertainment? How many different plays, movies, or books feature
characters with a physical or intellectual disability? Should art works such
as Pagliacci, where disabled characters are treated badly, still be
performed?

Lesson Plan Stems
When they think of opera, most people visualize either Wagner’s helmeted, MS/HS
warrior-maiden Brünnhilde, or the crying clown Pagliaccio, in a white pom-
pommed blouse, trousers, and conical hat. These images are both
stereotypes and archetypes. What is the difference between stereotype

Commedia dell’arte was a 16th-century Italian form of comedy that MS/HS
transcended social classes through the inclusion of stock characters,
standard plot lines, pantomime, popular songs, physical comedy, and rude
humor. Explore this art form, its costumes, customs, and symbols. How
has commedia dell’arte informed modern concepts of comedy? What other
famous operas or stories reference these same archetypal characters?
Analyze a popular comedy show or movie. Can you identify some traces of
commedia dell’arte in modern entertainments?
During the Prologue of Pagliacci, Tonio talks directly to the audience, HS
breaking through the “fourth wall” between the audience and the stage.
Others in the theater have tried to breach the “fifth wall” of distrust,
misunderstanding, and mutual patronizing between artists and critics. “The
Fifth Wall” has recently been used by critics of the Internet, saying that it
“closes in” users by discouraging normal social life. Compare/contrast the
uses of the terms fourth and fifth walls, and their implications for modern
society.
Clowns and fools are common motifs in literature. Explore the role of the HS
clown/fool in Pagliacci, then compare it to other works, such as:
 Shakespeare’s King Lear [1.4.146-8]
 The Great Dictator, a 1940 film directed by and starring Charlie
Chaplin, a satire of Adolf Hitler and Nazism
 A Night at the Opera, a 1935 film about a sly business manager
and two wacky friends of two opera singers who help them achieve
success while humiliating their stuffy and snobbish enemies.

Character Themes for Cavalleria rusticana and Pagliacci
Eleanore N. Childs, a Pittsburgh attorney and child advocate, worked with teachers for
more than a decade to research character education and develop the Heartwood
Ethics Curriculum for Children. Heartwood’s life lessons through literature promote
understanding and practice of seven universal character attributes, which constitute
the foundation of community among all people.
The Heartwood Institute defined seven universal character attributes:
 Courage is not only mastering fear; it is the ability to face difficulty or risk with integrity and
honor.
 Loyalty is being faithful to a person, group, or ideal based on understanding that we are all part
of something greater than ourselves.
 Justice is being guided by a balance of truth, reason, and fairness to all.
 Respect is regard for all life on earth. It calls us to value all people and their contributions,
viewpoints, and differences, such as culture, religion, age, race, and ability.
 Hope guides our future through faith and aspiration, helping us to rise above selfishness and
despair.
 Honesty is being truthful and honorable, the foundation for friendship and community.
 Love is deep care and concern for others, encompassing all the attributes. The more love we
give, the richer we become.
Discuss two attributes—justice and love—after reading the synopses of Cavalleria
rusticana and Pagliacci.
1. Based on their behaviors, how would each of the following characters define love?
 Men: Alfio, Turiddu, Canio, Taddeo
 Women: Lola, Santuzza, Nedda

you think men and women have different concepts of love today? If so, what are
they and why?
2. Each of these operas’ conflicts is resolved—for the moment—by public deaths. Do
the main characters in each opera believe justice has been achieved? Did the
actions of the characters deserve a public death? For whom has justice not been
served? Can two “wrongs” make a “right”?
3. In Cavalleria rusticana, Alfio and Turiddu duel over the infidelity of Alfio’s wife.
What kind of justice is represented by a duel? What are the rules of a duel? Why
do people think a formal duel will resolve a problem? What problem is Alfio hoping
to resolve with this duel? Are justice and honor the same idea?

both operas have to tell us about the nature of love? Do the men act out of true
love for their wives, or some other emotion?
