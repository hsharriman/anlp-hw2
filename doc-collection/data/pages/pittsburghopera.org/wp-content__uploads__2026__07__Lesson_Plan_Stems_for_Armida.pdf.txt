Lesson Plan Stems for Armida
How does the opera Armida by Joseph Haydn intersect with all academic content
areas? Begin with a lesson plan stem and connect the opera to classroom curricula.
NOTES: Suggested grade levels are listed to the right of each lesson plan stem.
PreK =Pre-Kindergarten, ES =Elementary school, MS =Middle school, HS =High school
Lesson Plan Stems in the English Language Arts and Mathematics
sections are Common Core Friendly, and are marked: CCF
Arts and Humanities
State Standards: 9.1, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4
Franz Joseph Haydn, the composer of Armida, was sometimes called “Papa” PreK
because he wrote so many symphonies. What are some other names used
for fathers?
The composer Haydn is also famous for his “Surprise Symphony.” What is a PreK
surprise? Have you ever been surprised? Is a surprise something good or
bad? Listen to Haydn’s “Surprise Symphony.” What is the surprise in this
music? How can we surprise people?
The Esterháza Palace, where Haydn lived and worked, includes a marionette PreK/ES
theater. What are marionettes? How are they different from puppets or
muppets?
In 1761, Haydn was hired by Prince Paul Anton as the Prince’s Kapellmeister ES/MS
and lived in the Prince’s Esterháza Palace. What is a palace? How does it
differ from other types of homes? What might it be like to live in a palace with
your boss? Brainstorm pros and cons of this arrangement.
Armida was sent to enchant Rinaldo because he was the bravest. What does ES/MS
it mean to be brave? What does it mean to be called the “most brave”? How
is bravery related to honor? Is it traditional in opera for the “bravest” to be the
tenor?
There are no chorus parts in Armida. Why did Haydn only include six singing ES/MS
roles and not chorus? How does this choice of limiting the number of roles
impact how the opera might be produced? How might this choice impact the
opera’s popularity?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 2

Lesson Plan Stems
Haydn’s Armida is often performed on period instruments. What does the ES/MS
term “period instrument” mean? Why would an orchestra want to use period
instruments? How would the sound differ from modern orchestral
instruments? How does an orchestra acquire period instruments for just a
few performances?
The enchantress Armida falls in love with Rinaldo, a Christian Knight of the ES/MS
First Crusade in the Middle Ages. What is a knight? Who can be a knight and
what does that title mean? What costumes come to mind when you picture a
knight? Research a medieval knight’s clothing and equipment. Design a
costume for Rinaldo to wear that makes his character clear to the audience.
What instruments would have comprised an orchestra in Haydn’s time? ES/MS
Trace the development of orchestral instruments from the 18th century to
now. Which instruments have changed the most, and which have hardly
changed? What instruments does a modern orchestra include?
Haydn’s Armida is scored for flute, two oboes, two clarinets, two bassoons, ES/MS
two horns/trumpets, timpani, strings, and continuo. What is a continuo? Was
this a typical selection of instruments in 1784? Would Haydn have used his
own orchestra at the Esterháza Court Theatre?
“Papa” Haydn was known as the “Father of the String Quartet” and as such ES/MS
was instrumental in the development of chamber music and the piano trio.
What is chamber music and what was its purpose? When and where would
chamber music originally have been performed? Would the music have been
composed for specific occasions?
Haydn wrote some coloratura passages in his operas and some of his music MS/HS
sounds similar to that of Mozart. Did Haydn borrow ideas from Mozart, or did
Mozart borrow from Haydn? Was Haydn familiar with Mozart’s operas? What
other musicians have “borrowed” musical ideas from other composers? What
is that kind of borrowing called when used in pop culture music?
When Haydn met Beethoven in Bonn in 1792, Haydn wrote in a letter that MS/HS
“Beethoven will one day be considered one of Europe’s greatest composers,
and I shall be proud to be called his teacher.” In retrospect, how flattering is
this tribute? How many other famous musicians (in any style of music) can
also claim famous teachers?
Why did Haydn compose so many works for baryton, a cello-like instrument MS/HS
that is no longer used? He also composed 32 pieces for a mechanical clock.
Why did he write music for such unusual instruments?
Armida’s United States’ 1981 premiere set the production during the Vietnam MS/HS
War, instead of the historical setting of the Middle Ages. What impact does
changing the setting have on this story, or how the audience receives the
story? Review the opera’s synopsis. Where and when would you choose to
set a production of Armida? What setting might resonate best with modern
audiences? Why?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 3

Lesson Plan Stems
At least six composers wrote operas based on Tasso’s epic poem MS/HS
Gerusalemme liberata: Lully’s 1686 Armide, Handel’s 1711 Rinaldo, Salieri’s
1771 Armida, Haydn’s 1784 Armida, Rossini’s 1817 Armida, and Dvořák’s
1904 Armida. Compare the characters and stories of several of these operas.
Would audiences have known the main tenets of the story before attending a
new opera based on this same poem? How might familiarity with the story
have affected their reactions? Do you enjoy seeing different productions of
stories you already know? How does this familiarity impact your reaction?
Why might this story have been so popular?
Haydn experimented with prevailing stylistic trends during his lifetime. Listen MS/HS
to some of his earlier music and compare it to Baroque music. Listen to some
later music and compare to the elegant musical style typical of Austrian
music. Listen to some later music, similar in style to Carl Philipp Emanuel
Bach. How did Haydn fuse these styles into his own musical identity?
Esterháza Palace, where Haydn was Kapellmeister, is sometimes called the MS/HS
“Hungarian Versailles” and it is Hungary's grandest Rococo edifice. What is
Versailles? What is meant by the term “Rococo”? What design elements
define the Rococo period? The palace includes two opera houses and a
marionette theater. Why did Prince Nikolaus include a marionette theater?
Do other palaces also include marionette theaters?
Why do contemporary arts organizations, such as symphonies and operas, MS/HS
need support beyond ticket sales? What percentage of a performing arts
organization’s budget is supported by ticket sales? What kind of patronage
supported Haydn’s composing and performing? How has that kind of support
changed in modern times?

State Standards: 13.1, 13.2, 13.3, 13.4
What kind of music do you like? If you could be a professional musician, what PreK
instrument would you play, or would you want to sing? Can you name some
famous musicians or singers?
Haydn’s reputation was someone who “played well with others” when he led PreK
the Esterháza court musicians. What does it mean to play well with others?
How can we play well with others?
Living in the royal palace meant that Haydn did a lot of his work alone. When PreK
is it best to do our work all by ourselves? When should we ask to work with
others?
Haydn came from a poor, working class background, and his success can be ES/MS
called a “rags-to-riches” tale. What does it mean if someone has a rags-to-
riches story? What other famous people might also qualify as “rags-to-riches”?
How did Haydn build his path to success?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 4

Lesson Plan Stems
What type of craftspeople might be involved in producing an opera? Consider ES/MS
the elements involved in making an opera: designing and constructing sets,
building costumes, playing music, singing, dancing, lighting, and so forth. What
training or professional development and certifications would these
craftspeople need to have to be hired for this opera’s production?
Create a timeline of Haydn’s training and career, beginning with his nine-year ES/MS
apprenticeship in a choir school. What are the important “watershed” moments
that helped Haydn build his success? Based on your timeline, would you
describe Haydn as a self-taught musician? How does Haydn’s career path
compare to that of modern musicians? What elements or experiences do they
have in common? What advantages or disadvantages mark each musician’s
career path?
Securing a patron was a crucial step for artists in Haydn’s time, and Haydn MS/HS
spent much of his career as a court musician for the wealthy Esterházy family.
What is a patron? What did the Esterházy patronage mean for his career? Do
patrons still exist for modern artists, and do they hold the same significance for
careers in the arts now?
Esterháza Palace was the home of Joseph Haydn and his orchestra from 1766 MS/HS
to 1790. What were the careers like of the orchestra members? Did they have
artistic and personal freedom? Did they have vacations? Did they make their
own meals? How could they perform so many concerts in a year? If you were
to organize the Esterháza orchestra, what might you write into a union
document?
What skills are needed to be a manager or supervisor? As Kapellmeister, MS/HS
Haydn supervised the Prince’s musicians, and by all accounts was a well-
respected boss: he maintained a cordial working atmosphere and effectively
represented the musicians' interests with their employer. How did Haydn learn
those skills? What other traits are needed to be a skilled manager? Where are
these skills and traits learned nowadays?
What is copyright, and how does it apply to music? In Haydn’s day, copyright MS/HS
laws were just coming into being, and few fraud and pirating protections were
available to musicians or publishers. What protections are available for
musicians? What are the rules around using someone’s work as part of a new
piece, such as “sampling”? Explore the rules for copyright and the protections
they provide.
Working at the Esterházy Palace in the country meant that Haydn spent much MS/HS
of his career isolated from other composers and trends in music. This
isolation—as he put it—“forced (him) to become original." Are many successful
people “isolated” in their work? Do you think isolation would help in one’s
career? Many schools nowadays emphasize working in teams with their
students; how does this notion work with Haydn’s belief that his lack of
exposure to new ideas made him more creative?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 5

Lesson Plan Stems
Investigate the career trajectory for a young theater design student. What MS/HS
training and skill sets are needed to enter this profession? What experiences
would help to secure contracts? What are some real-world lessons to learn
that might not be taught in the classroom? How can this experience be
gained?
The patronage system offered opportunities for abuse as well as success MS/HS
since employment conditions were subject to a patron’s whims. Haydn’s
“Farewell” Finale of Symphony #45 shows a clever protest against one such
instance when Prince Nikolaus kept his musicians at the palace longer than
expected. In this Finale, musicians stopped playing one-by-one, snuffed out
their candles, and walked off stage, leaving only Haydn and his concertmaster
playing at the end. Apparently the theatrics worked, as everyone was allowed
to return home to Eisenstadt the next day. What similarities can you find
between union organizers’ methods and the tale of Haydn’s “Farewell”
Symphony? Was his musical work meant to be humorous only, or do you think
Haydn was deliberately taking action to make a point with his patron? Would
you call what Haydn did a “strike”?
Civics and Government
State Standards: 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4
Esterháza Palace was built by a prince. Do we have princes in the United PreK
States? Who is the head of the United States government?
Haydn wrote a melody that became so popular it was used in Germany’s PreK/ES
national anthem. What song is the United States’ national anthem? Can you
hum or sing a verse of our anthem?
In 1797 Haydn wrote the “Emperor’s Hymn” and this melody became so ES/MS
popular that it was used as the tune for Germany’s national anthem. What is
an anthem, and how is it different from other patriotic songs? What is the
purpose of having an anthem? Does every country have an anthem?
Consider the United States anthem: “The Star Spangled Banner.” Look at the
words; why was this song chosen to become our national anthem?
Armida is set during the First Crusade. Which countries banded together to ES/MS
“liberate” Jerusalem? Who was in charge of the crusader army? What was
the command structure for a crusade? How was that decided? How was the
First Crusade funded?
Armida is set in Jerusalem during the First Crusade. Trace the history of MS/HS
Jerusalem’s government and control by different groups of people and
religious sects. What shaped the principles of the various governments that
have controlled Jerusalem? Why did invading armies feel justified in
conquering Jerusalem? Which government is in control of the city today?

Armida is adapted from Torquato Tasso’s poem Gerusalemme liberata; this MS/HS

What is an epic poem? Read a translation of the original epic poem by HS
Torquato Tasso, called Gerusalemme liberata. Compare his version to
several libretto adaptations. Why do you think this story has been used in
more than 100 operas and ballets? What makes it so well suited to these art
forms?
What is a stock character, and how do stock characters differ from HS

Character Themes for Armida
Eleanore N. Childs, a Pittsburgh attorney and child advocate, worked with teachers for
more than a decade to research character education and develop the Heartwood Ethics
Curriculum for Children. Heartwood’s life lessons through literature promote
understanding and practice of seven universal character attributes, which constitute the
foundation of community among all people.
The Heartwood Institute defined seven universal character attributes:
 Courage is not only mastering fear; it is the ability to face difficulty or risk with integrity and honor.
 Loyalty is being faithful to a person, group, or ideal based on understanding that we are all part
of something greater than ourselves.
 Justice is being guided by a balance of truth, reason, and fairness to all.
 Respect is regard for all life on earth. It calls us to value all people and their contributions,
viewpoints, and differences, such as culture, religion, age, race, and ability.
 Hope guides our future through faith and aspiration, helping us to rise above selfishness and
despair.
 Honesty is being truthful and honorable, the foundation for friendship and community.
 Love is deep care and concern for others, encompassing all the attributes. The more love we
give, the richer we become.
Discuss two attributes—LOVE and LOYALTY—after reading the synopsis of Armida.
1. Knowing she has cast a spell on Rinaldo to divert him from his conquest, do you
think Armida really loves Rinaldo? How would Armida define love? What do her
final actions reveal—love, jealousy, vengeance, or another attribute?
2. Can someone be tricked into loving another person? Can love result from deceit?
How are desire and obsession different from love? What does this story’s use of
love as manipulation tell us about the nature of love in this opera?
3. To whom is Armida really loyal? Is she using her love to serve her country or
herself? How do her final actions influence ideas about her concept of loyalty? To
whom is Rinaldo loyal? Is his loyalty stronger than his desire?
4. Did Rinaldo ever really love Armida? What do his final actions toward her reveal?
Did loyalty to his cause overcome love, or is he demonstrating a different
character attribute?
