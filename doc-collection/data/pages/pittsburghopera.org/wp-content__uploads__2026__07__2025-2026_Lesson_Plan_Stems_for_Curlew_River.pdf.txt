What is the difference between an opera, a play, and a ballet? Choose a PreK/ES

What is good audience behavior—also called etiquette—for attending a PreK/ES

Curlew River is a reimagining of a famous Noh play. Noh theater artists PreK
use fans as props. What are some different kinds of fans? When would
someone use a fan?
Look at a picture of the Benedum Theater’s stage and compare it to a PreK
picture of the stage at the National Noh Theater in Tokyo. What do both

will be staged in the Calvary Episcopal Church in Shadyside. Look at a

to a theater? How is it different?
Props in Noh theater are minimal. The most commonly used prop is ES
the fan, as it is carried by all performers regardless of role. Artists may
carry their fan in hand when entering the stage or carry it tucked into
an obi (a sash). What are some common props used in opera? Who is
responsible for keeping track of props?
Read the summary of Curlew River, and listen to some selections from the ES
opera. Draw caricatures of the characters based on their personalities, as
they come through in the music.
Benjamin Britten was influenced by Noh theater and wrote Curlew River for ES
an all-male cast. He also composed Billy Budd for an all-male cast. Are
there other Britten operas with female characters?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 2

Lesson Plan Stems
As with many of Britten's works, individual instruments in Curlew River are ES
used to symbolize particular characters. Listen to several music samples
from this opera. How does Britten use the flute and horn to symbolize The
Madwoman and The Ferryman?
Noh masks are treasured by Noh families and institutions, and the most ES
valuable Noh masks are held in private collections, rarely seen by the
public. Using a photograph, recreate a Noh mask as accurately as
possible. If you were to design a mask to represent an archetypal Western
character, such as a cowboy, a farmer, a princess, etc., what would that
mask look like?
The story of Curlew River is told as a mystery play performed by monks ES
portraying the four main characters: The Abbot (who acts as a narrator),
The Madwoman, The Ferryman, and The Traveller. A chorus is provided
by eight Pilgrims. What is the highest note in Curlew River? Chart the voice
ranges of the characters: 1 treble, 4 tenors, 5 baritones, 3 basses.
Noh theater is a classical Japanese musical drama that has been ES/MS
performed since the 14th century. Noh combines dance, drama, music, and
poetry, and is characterized by its highly stylized and symbolic nature, with
actors wearing elaborate masks and costumes, and using slow, precise
movements to convey emotion and narrative. Noh plays are typically
performed alongside Kyogen, a form of comedic theatre. Does Noh have a
Western culture counterpart? What are some theater traditions in other
parts of the world?
After two decades of writing more conventional-styled operas, Britten’s ES/MS
next three stage works were called Parables for Church Performance:
dramas specifically designed to be performed in church. What is unique
about using a church as an opera venue? How does a church setting differ
from a theater setting? What might be the challenges of writing an opera
for a very specific type of space?
The composer Benjamin Britten also wrote A Young Person’s Guide to the ES/MS
Orchestra, a piece originally commissioned for the British educational
documentary film called Instruments of the Orchestra released in
November 1946. The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra is scored for
symphony orchestra. Listen to this piece, then compare it to the orchestra
Britten uses in Curlew River. Compare the size, number, and types of
instruments used in The Young Person’s Guide to the Orchestra to the
orchestra used in Curlew River: How are they alike? How do they differ?
Why do you think Britten chose a different kind of orchestra for Curlew
River?
Benjamin Britten collaborated with librettist William Plomer to create ES/MS
Curlew River. Read the summary of Plomer’s life. Would Plomer have
been familiar with Noh theater? How does Curlew River reflect Noh
traditions?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 3

Lesson Plan Stems
The harp part in Curlew River is heavily influenced by music written for the ES/MS
koto, which is a type of zither, and the chamber organ part features
extensive use of tone clusters, which are derived from the shō, an ancient
Japanese free-reed mouth organ. What makes an instrument a zither?
What makes an instrument a mouth-organ? Explore other world
instruments in the zither and the mouth organ families.
Though written just two years after his epic composition War Requiem, Op. ES/MS
66, which uses two orchestras, two choruses, and three soloists, Britten’s
Curlew River’s orchestra includes only seven instruments. Another
difference is that the casts of his Church Parables are all male. Why would
a composer write for a cast of all the same gender? In Curlew River the
role of The Madwoman is written for a tenor voice. Why is The Madwoman
played by a man? How does this help or challenge the characterization of
The Madwoman? Britten’s Church Parables are heavily influenced by Noh
theater. Are all parts in Noh male?
Curlew River is based on the Japanese Noh play Sumidagawa, which ES/MS
Britten saw during a visit to Japan in early 1956. In a broadcast message
to the people of Japan following his visit, Britten declared that he had come
to regard Noh plays as “among the greatest theatrical experiences of my
life”. What other artists have been influenced greatly by one theatrical
experience?
Benjamin Britten composed Curlew River and conducted the premiere ES/MS
recording, even though the opera does not require a traditional conductor
because the instrumentalists conduct themselves. Is it common for
composers to conduct the premiere of their works today?
Benjamin Britten composed a variety of stage works for ballet, operetta, ES/MS
opera, comic opera, children’s opera, miracle play, and parables for church
performance. Do most composers have such a range of works? What
other composers have also written a broad range of work?
Noh actors wear elaborate silk costumes called shozoku (robes) that can MS/HS
be considered works of art, along with wigs, hats, and props such as the
fan. Opera costumes may also be elaborate but are usually built for
specific productions and from a director’s artistic “vision.” Compare how
costumes are used in Noh theater to how they are used in Western opera.
What are the seven categories of Noh costumes? How does a Noh
performer create character with his or her costume? How is character
created through Western costume design? Investigate some different kinds
of Noh costumes. Design some Western opera costumes that contain Noh
elements.
Benjamin Britten was a 20th-century composer. What are characteristics of MS/HS
20th-century music? How does it compare with earlier time periods? Listen
to excerpts of Benjamin Britten’s opera Curlew River. What musical style
best describes Britten’s compositions, and why?

Lesson Plan Stems
Compare audience expectations and etiquette for watching a Western MS/HS
opera in a Western-style theater to an audience watching a Japanese Noh
performance in a Noh theater. How do both audiences typically behave in
a formal theater? How do they know when the performance has begun?
How do they know what is happening on stage? How does each audience
show its appreciation for the performance? How might a change in a
performance’s location impact how an audience behaves or patrons’
expectations for the performance? For Curlew River, the audience will be
viewing this production at Calvary Episcopal Church. How might this
religious setting change the etiquette an audience usually follows for a
theatrical performance in a secular setting?
Noh is a chanted drama, and a few commentators have dubbed it MS/HS
"Japanese opera". However, the singing in Noh involves a limited tonal
range, with lengthy, repetitive passages in a narrow dynamic range. Could
a trained western opera singer learn to sing in Noh? How does Noh
training differ from Western opera training?
Britten opens Curlew River with the plainsong “Te lucis ante terminum” by MS/HS
Thomas Tallis, who is also considered one of England’s greatest
composers. Why would a composer use another composer’s music in his
opera? Did Benjamin Britten use the music of Tallis out of respect? What is
meant by the term “plainsong”? What is the history of chanting and
plainsong? How is it related to Gregorian chant? Look at the sample of
Tallis’s “Te lucis . . .” printed below. How different from modern music
notation is this example of plainsong?
Listen to a recording of traditional koto music and then the harp part in MS/HS
Curlew River. How did Britten adapt the sounds of the Japanese
instrument to a western instrument? How would the playing technique
need to change on the harp? Are the timbres similar? Repeat the activity
with a traditional Japanese flute (shakuhachi) and a western flute. How do
special techniques (such as flutter-tonguing and glissandi) help
characterize The Madwoman in Curlew River?
One of the most notable features of a Noh stage is the independent roof MS/HS
that hangs over the stage, even in indoor theatres. Supported by four
columns, the roof symbolizes the sanctity of the stage, as its design is
derived from the worship pavilion (haiden) or sacred dance pavilion
(kagura-den) of Shinto shrines. The roof also unifies the theatre space and
defines the stage as an architectural entity. Compare this stage tradition to
the proscenium and scenery setup at the Benedum Center for an opera
performance.
Pittsburgh Opera Education 5

Lesson Plan Stems
In Curlew River, there is no conductor specified—instead, the musicians HS
lead themselves and the places indicating which instrument is to lead are
marked in the score. This approach allows Britten’s performers to play in
two or more separate groups at separate tempi, much like the music of a
Nobayashi ensemble in Noh plays. This approach leads to another unusual
notational device, the “Curlew sign”, which is used to “resynchronize”
musicians by instructing one to sustain or repeat notes ad lib until a given
point has been reached in the music of another group. What does the
“Curlew sign” look like in a score? How is this improvisation similar to jazz?

Character Themes for Curlew River
Eleanore N. Childs, a Pittsburgh attorney and child advocate, worked with
teachers for more than a decade to research character education and develop the
Heartwood Ethics Curriculum for Children. Heartwood’s life lessons through
literature promote understanding and practice of seven universal character
attributes, which constitute the foundation of community among all people.
The Heartwood Institute defined seven universal character attributes:
• Courage is not only mastering fear; it is the ability to face difficulty or risk with integrity and
honor.
• Loyalty is being faithful to a person, group, or ideal based on understanding that we are all
part of something greater than ourselves.
• Justice is being guided by a balance of truth, reason, and fairness to all.
• Respect is regard for all life on earth. It calls us to value all people and their contributions,
viewpoints, and differences, such as culture, religion, age, race, and ability.
• Hope guides our future through faith and aspiration, helping us to rise above selfishness
and despair.
• Honesty is being truthful and honorable, the foundation for friendship and community.
• Love is deep care and concern for others, encompassing all the attributes. The more love
we give, the richer we become.
Discuss two attributes—hope and love—after reading the synopsis of Curlew
River.
1. The attribute of love would encompass the quality of compassion. What is
compassion? How is compassion shown in the actions of the characters toward
The Madwoman?
2. If one thematic idea in Curlew River is the concept of an individual at odds with
his or her society, how does the attribute of hope apply in this opera? Who has
hope, and for what? Is that hope realized by the end of the opera? Does a
different message of hope transcend the initial desires of the characters?
3. In this opera, monks are making a pilgrimage based on their faith. In this
context, what role does faith have in this opera, and how does the characters’
collective faith contribute to, or demonstrate, the attributes of hope and love?
