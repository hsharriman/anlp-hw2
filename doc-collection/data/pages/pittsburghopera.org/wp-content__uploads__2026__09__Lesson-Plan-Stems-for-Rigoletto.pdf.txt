Lesson Plan Stems for Rigoletto
How does Rigoletto by Giuseppe Verdi and Francesco Maria Piave
intersect with all academic content areas?
Begin with a lesson plan stem and connect the opera to classroom curricula.
NOTES: Suggested grade levels are listed to the right of each lesson plan stem.
PreK = Pre-Kindergarten, ES = elementary school, MS = middle school, HS = high school
Arts and Humanities
State Standards: 9.1, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4
Listen to “La Donna è Mobile” from Act III. Can you tap along with the beats PreK
or rhythm of this song? The beats of a song are also called its meter. How
many beats do you feel in this song’s meter?
Tap out the beat (also called meter) for ¾ time. Demonstrate the meter by PreK
moving to it in your own unique way, such as using scarves to “wave to” the
rhythm. Which song from Rigoletto uses ¾ time?
What is a jester? Do you think a jester is different from a clown, and if so, in PreK
what ways?
“La donna è mobile” is a song about how women change their minds. Create PreK
new lyrics for "La donna è mobile." What will your lyrics discuss? Make sure
that your lyrics use the same rhythm and musical accentuation as Verdi’s.
Rigoletto has a famous quartet: “Bella figlia dell’amore.” In a quartet, how PreK
many people are singing together? In a trio? In a duet? Listen to the quartet
in this opera. Can you understand what the four singers are singing? If there
were a quartet in a play, could you understand what everyone was saying?
Write a memory about a time when you were in a disguise. How did other PreK/ES
people respond to your costume? How did being in disguise feel?
Imagine you have a hunchback like Rigoletto. How would a person with this PreK/ES
physical problem move? How would you express this disability in a dance?
Listen to the Act III quartet in Rigoletto. As you listen, show how many people PreK/ES
are singing simultaneously by holding up one, two, three, or four fingers.
When are all four characters singing together?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 2

Lesson Plan Stems
Listen to excerpts from the opera. Can you distinguish between different ES
types of ensemble singing, such as quartets, duets, and trios?
Quartets in opera often include each character introducing a solo line before ES
all four joining together. Why might quartets begin with the solo lines? Can
you understand what each character is singing and expressing? Could you
understand what four characters were saying simultaneously in a spoken
play?
The Rigoletto full orchestral score calls for a cimbasso. What is a cimbasso, ES
and how is it played? In modern orchestras, a tuba is often used instead.
What kind of sound does a tuba make? Compare the cimbasso to a modern
tuba. What might a cimbasso have sounded like? Do cimbassos still exist?
Compare images of the hunchback in Rigoletto to the images of The ES
Hunchback of Notre-Dame. Create your own drawing of how you believe the
character should look. Why do you think the hunchback should look the way
you’ve drawn the character?
The Rigoletto chorus has at least 16 singers—tenors and baritones. Why did ES
Verdi only include men in the chorus?
Verdi's operas are frequently staged around the world. Explore how many of ES
his operas are currently being produced in theaters around the world. Why
are Verdi’s operas so popular?
The music of Rigoletto has been used in Grand Theft Auto soundtracks and ES
Doritos commercials. What other popular uses for the music can you find?
In 1813, the year Verdi was born, the waltz dominated European ballrooms. ES
What is the time signature of a waltz? How does it compare to other common
time signatures? Tap out the waltz meter (STRONG-weak-weak) with your
hands or another instrument. Learn the basic steps to a waltz.
The bicentenary of Verdi's birth in 2013 was celebrated around the world, ES
both in performances and broadcasts. What other artists have been
celebrated for their 100th , 150th, or 200th birthdays? In what other ways have
famous artists been celebrated historically?
In 2011, Anthony Tommasini ranked Verdi eighth on his list of the ten ES
greatest composers of classical music. Who would be in your top ten list of
composers? Why? Which artists do you think will still be known or performed
200 years from now?
Look up the words “prelude” and “overture” in the dictionary. Compare the ES/MS
two terms. Why do you think Verdi used “prelude” in Rigoletto rather than
“overture”?
Listen to several parts of the prelude that demonstrate certain themes or ES/MS
motifs in the opera. Given a summary of each act, explain which part of the
prelude might signal or match which event or idea in the story.

Lesson Plan Stems

Aware that the melody he wrote for the Duke's aria "La donna è mobile" MS
("Woman is fickle") would become a popular hit, Verdi excluded it from
orchestral rehearsals for the opera and rehearsed the song with the tenor
separately. Do opera premieres require secrecy today? Why or why not?
What might be the advantage of such a tactic? Are there any drawbacks to
this kind of secrecy? What other performers tend to keep their new shows
“under wraps” while rehearsing? What does this do for a new performance?
What are some Broadway musical melodies that were kept secret until
openings and have since become “ear worms”?
The prelude to Rigoletto is a kind of synopsis of the opera’s dramatic MS
essentials. The brass, led by solo trumpet and trombone, play a motif
associated with the curse placed on Rigoletto. This movement builds in
intensity and eventually explodes into a passionate sobbing figure for full
orchestra; the figure peters out, the brass motif returns, and simple cadences
bring the prelude to a solemn close. Listen carefully to the prelude for these
elements and mark each of these sections in the score.
If an opera company chooses to update Rigoletto and use contemporary MS
costumes, how does that impact the audience’s understanding of the story?
Could this be a modern story? Without traditional costumes, is any meaning
lost?
Since Rigoletto’s premiere, the opera has never left its place in the standard MS/HS
repertory of opera houses worldwide. Is that typical? Why has Rigoletto
remained popular?

In March 1843, Verdi visited Vienna to oversee a production of Nabucco, MS
where Gaetano Donizetti was musical director. The older composer
recognized Verdi's talent, and wrote in a letter: "I am very, very happy to give
way to people of talent like Verdi . . . Nothing will prevent the good Verdi
from soon reaching one of the most honorable positions in the cohort of
composers." How significant is praise from one composer to another? What
might that public praise do for someone’s career?
Verdi's gift for music was already apparent by 1820–1821 when he began MS

Character Themes for Rigoletto
Eleanore N. Childs, a Pittsburgh attorney and child advocate, worked with
teachers for more than a decade to research character education and develop the
Heartwood Ethics Curriculum for Children. Heartwood’s life lessons through
literature promote understanding and practice of seven universal character
attributes, which constitute the foundation of community among all people.
The Heartwood Institute defined seven universal character attributes:
 Courage is not only mastering fear; it is the ability to face difficulty or risk with integrity and
honor.
 Loyalty is being faithful to a person, group, or ideal based on understanding that we are all
part of something greater than ourselves.
 Justice is being guided by a balance of truth, reason, and fairness to all.
 Respect is regard for all life on earth. It calls us to value all people and their contributions,
viewpoints, and differences, such as culture, religion, age, race, and ability.
 Hope guides our future through faith and aspiration, helping us to rise above selfishness
and despair.
 Honesty is being truthful and honorable, the foundation for friendship and community.
 Love is deep care and concern for others, encompassing all the attributes. The more love
we give, the richer we become.
Discuss two attributes—Justice and Love—after reading the synopsis of
Rigoletto.
1. In the opera, how does Rigoletto show his affection for Gilda? Does Rigoletto
truly love his daughter? Does she love him in return? Explain your conclusions.
2. By the end of the opera, Monterone’s curse has been fulfilled. What kind of
justice has been served, if any? Do Gilda and Rigoletto deserve their fates?
What should happen at the end of this opera to truly serve justice?
3. What do the following characters from the opera imagine love to be: the Duke
of Mantua, Countess Ceprano, Rigoletto, Gilda, and Maddalena. Which
character’s concept of love comes closest to the Heartwood Institute definition
of love?
4. Why does Gilda die for the Duke? Do you think she does this to demonstrate
her love or is it her concept of justice that prompts her to enter the inn when
she does?
