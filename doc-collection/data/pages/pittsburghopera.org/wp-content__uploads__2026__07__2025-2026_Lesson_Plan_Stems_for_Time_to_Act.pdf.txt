Lesson Plan Stems for Time to Act
How does Time to Act by Laura Kaminsky and Crystal Manich
intersect with all academic content areas?
Begin with a lesson plan stem and connect the opera to classroom curricula.
NOTES: Suggested grade levels are listed to the right of each lesson plan stem.
PreK = Pre-Kindergarten, ES = elementary school, MS = middle school, HS = high school
Arts and Humanities
State Standards: 9.1, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4
Time to Act is set in a school. What do we expect to see in a school or a PreK
classroom? What makes a school recognizable as a school to everyone in
an audience?
A librettist is someone who writes words to sing in an opera. Write some PreK
words about something that happened at your school and sing them to tell
the story.
Some operas, like Time to Act, have spoken lines, but most are sung. PreK
Practice saying the same sentence in your whispering voice, your talking
voice, and your singing voice.
At the beginning of Time to Act, Tyson shakes hands with a drummer. PreK
What are some percussion instruments? Can you name some different
kinds of drums and instruments that are played with mallets?
At the end of an opera, the performers appear together for a “curtain call,” PreK/ES
which is when the audience shows its appreciation for the show. How do
audiences show appreciation for performers? How do artists thank
audiences during a curtain call? How would you organize a curtain call for
this opera? Which characters will go first? Last? Why? Practice giving a
curtain call with your classmates.
Conductors lead musicians by showing them when and how to play the PreK/ES
music together. How does a conductor show the musicians what tempo to
play? Imagine you are conducting an orchestra for an opera. How would
you keep the orchestra on tempo? Show how you might guide an orchestra
to change from one tempo to another.
Pittsburgh Opera Education 2

Lesson Plan Stems
In many operas important action occurs offstage. What important actions ES
have occurred off-stage in Time to Act? How and when does the audience
know what happened?
In Time to Act, the sound effects include an Intercom Voice and Safety Drill ES
Alarms. Who prepares the sound effects, and how are they controlled
during a performance?
The cast for Time to Act includes: ES
Alona, Mezzo, the new girl
Tyson, Baritone, an injured football player
Bailey, Soprano, a responsible and sweet spirit
José, Tenor, an actor and Alona's defender
Robin Grace, Mezzo or Baritone, the drama teacher
Chorus of 8, the other Playmakers
Is having a cast of 13 considered a small number for operas? Is writing an
opera that depicts high school students unusual?
Some of the libretto lines in Time to Act are to be spoken and some are ES/MS
meant to be sung. What markings appear in the libretto to indicate which is
which? How do artists project spoken lines?
In Time to Act, there are times when multiple characters sing/speak at the ES/MS
same time. How are those sections of the libretto marked? How are they
rehearsed?
Professional opera singers are usually at least in their twenties, yet the ES/MS
artists in Time to Act are cast as high school students. How would a stage
director ensure that the characters come across as their ages should be?
The set for Time to Act features a “typical” linoleum floor that might be ES/MS
found in a school setting. The linoleum floor for this opera is actually just
painted to look like linoleum. Study some linoleum patterns and make
samples for a set design.
Crystal Manich, the Time to Act librettist, has spent much of her career as ES/MS
a stage director. How is an opera stage director different from a musical
director?
Time to Act is a brand-new opera. Who chooses the composer and ES/MS
librettist when a new opera is created? Do some creators decide to write
an opera on their own? Do some operas have an impresario and that
person chooses who will create the work?
Listen to some selections from Time to Act and Hamilton. What are some ES/MS
musical elements in Time to Act that are similar to those in the musical
Hamilton? Are the definitions of musical theater and opera blurring?
The Time to Act chorus includes two sopranos, two mezzos, two tenors, MS/HS
and two baritones. Do the singers need to blend or should individual voices
be heard? Compare this chorus to one of a larger opera, such as Falstaff.
Pittsburgh Opera Education 3

Lesson Plan Stems
Time to Act includes elements of musical styles other than classical opera, MS/HS
such as jazz and hip-hop. In the opening scene, a drummer plays a bee-
bop riff. What is bee-bop? Additionally, some characters improvise lines—
“riff” or “scat”—to demonstrate free-flowing ideas. What does it mean to
scat sing? What is a riff? Practice scatting over a rhythmic line. Listen to
some jazz singers (like Louis Armstrong) who scat. What other jazz artists
used scat singing?
Operas often contain small parts for characters that may be necessary for MS/HS
only little pieces of the story, such as the role of Bailey and the Chorus in
Time to Act. How would you prepare if you were hired to play a “bit part” in
a professional production as opposed to a school production? Debate the
saying: “There are no small parts, only small actors.”
Four of the principal singers in Time to Act perform some of their lines as MS/HS
characters from the Greek play Antigone:
Alona as Antigone
Tyson as Creon
Bailey as Ismene
José as Haemon
How would the principals change their delivery to indicate when they are
performing their roles as high schoolers and when they are performing
their roles as actors in Antigone?
Pittsburgh Opera’s production of Time to Act will be a world premiere. How MS/HS
prestigious is that? Does an American premiere have as much
importance? Why or why not?
Time to Act uses a lot of percussion instruments. Does that mirror the MS/HS
instrumentation in musical theater? Compare a typical Mozart opera
orchestra to those of Verdi or Puccini. Does any composer write for those
kinds of orchestras today?
Creating a new opera involves “workshopping” the libretto and the music, HS
i.e. trying them out with live artists to solicit feedback and suggestions.
Some opera composers, like Puccini, wrote multiple versions of operas,
even after the premieres. Does having a live audience help with changes?
How do creators decide when a new work is “done”?
Laura Kaminsky, Time to Act’s composer, was trained classically. HS
Discuss how her music would be accepted by classically trained musicians
and hip-hop artists. Would using hip-hop style be considered cultural
misappropriation?

Career Education and Work
State Standards: 13.1, 13.2, 13.3, 13.4
A stage director decides what actors should do and how they move on PreK
stage. Stage direct a scene from a children’s book, such as The
Gingerbread Man. How should the character move?
In Time to Act, an intercom is heard directing students for a drill. What is an PreK
intercom? What kinds of announcements are made? Is it important to listen
to those messages?
Crystal Manich, the Time to Act librettist, has also directed a circus. What is PreK
a circus? What do characters do in a circus? What safety hazards might
performers in a circus encounter? How can circus performers rehearse so
they are safe?
In Time to Act, the Intercom Voice calls someone to the office. Who has the ES
job of speaking on the Intercom in this opera? Who makes the intercom
announcements at your school? How can someone learn to speak clearly on
a microphone? What jobs require the use of microphones?
The character of José believes a lead role in his school’s play might help ES/MS
him get into Carnegie Mellon University. What is Carnegie Mellon? Why
would José want to go there if he wants to be an actor? What other schools
might help José realize his dream of acting?
Laura Kaminsky and Crystal Manich created Time to Act through ES/MS
professional collaboration. What does it mean to collaborate? What other
professions rely on collaboration? What other professions—both on- and off-
stage—collaborate to make an opera performance? Select one of those
professions and research what requirements are needed to work in that job.
Crystal Manich, the Time to Act librettist, has spent much of her career as a ES/MS
stage director. Is it unusual for someone to do both? What other career
paths do other opera librettists follow?
Investigate one of the following professions in opera: the stage manager, the ES/MS
conductor, or a principal artist. How does someone qualify to work at this
profession? What education and experience are needed to land this job?
What are your responsibilities? What are the benefits and drawbacks of
working in this position? For the position you’ve chosen, create a bulleted list
of “To Dos” for the day before a performance.
Think about how often you hear music as part of another experience, such ES/MS
as when you see an advertisement, or watch a movie, or enter a business.
What types of companies need someone to compose music for their
products or productions? What other opportunities exist for professional
composers? Do you think people who write music for a living are in
demand? Why or why not?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 5

Lesson Plan Stems
As Time to Act was being created, the composer and librettist got help from ES/MS
a dramaturgist, Amy Hutchison. What is dramaturgy and how does someone
train to become a dramaturgist?
Does having a World Premiere listed on a resume help someone gain ES/MS
notoriety or more opportunities? Why or why not? Do performers have
resumes, or do they use something else? Who in an opera company is
responsible for hiring performers? What are some items on a performer’s
resume that must be included? If someone is new to professional
performance, what items would be expected for their resume, as opposed to
someone who has been performing professionally for many years?
One of Laura Kaminsky’s operas, As One, is among the most produced MS
contemporary operas since its 2014 premiere, with 60+ productions to date
across the U.S., Europe, Canada, South America, and Australia. Why would
she choose to list this information in her bio and resume?
Sophocles, the author of the play the students in Time to Act are rehearsing, MS/HS
was very wealthy, both through inheritance and his highly successful career
as a playwright and public official in Athens. He wrote 123 plays, and most
of them won first prizes in dramatic competitions. What are some lucrative
competitions today? In the age of the internet, is prestige worth as much as
prize money?
Librettist Crystal Manich is an Emmy Award nominated, internationally- MS/HS
renowned creative director and writer whose works for stage, film, and circus
have been seen across the United States, Europe, Argentina, and Australia.
She has held several Artistic Director positions, and served as adjunct
faculty at UCLA and Rider University. She is represented by United Talent
Agency. How does someone choose what to include in a bio? How
important is it for a professional creative to have an agent or agency?
How did composer Laura Kaminsky and librettist Crystal Manich apply for HS
and receive commissions to develop Time to Act? Must contemporary
creators seek out funding? From where does funding come for writing
contemporary pieces?
Civics and Government
State Standards: 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4
One of the props used in this opera is a crown. What is a crown? Who PreK
wears it, and why? What should it look like?
Sophocles, the ancient Greek author who wrote Antigone, was a citizen of PreK
ancient Athens, and as such, would have been allowed to vote. What is
voting? Why is it done? Practice voting on an issue in your class so that
everyone’s vote is included.
Pittsburgh Opera Education 6

Lesson Plan Stems
Some schools practice fire drills to make sure students know what to do in PreK
case there ever is a real fire. Who decides when fire drills happen?
In Time to Act, students are rehearing a play from Ancient Greece. Ancient PreK/ES
Greece had city-states with various kinds of governments. What kind of
government does the United States have? Who is the head of the United
States government, and what do we call that role? Who is the head of your
state’s government, and what is that position called?
In Time to Act, the characters say that “Creon makes the law to serve ES
himself.” What kind of government would that be called, where the ruler
makes laws that suit the ruler?
In the Time to Act libretto, “Robin claps to get the attention of the room. ES/MS
Things start to quiet down as the Playmakers pile their phones into a
basket.” Why would students need to put their cell phones in a basket? Do
schools allow students to use cell phones during class? Do students
comply with this rule?
In the Time to Act libretto, an Intercom Voice says, “This is an active ES/MS
shooter drill, prepare for lockdown.” Should the government require
schools to practice active shooter drills? Should schools teach students
how to react to emergencies? What does “Code Red” mean? Does your
school practice these drills? What differences did you notice between the
staged drill and the ones you practice at school?
In Time to Act, José says: ES/MS
Can a drill prevent a crime?
Like Creon’s law, reality isn’t reflected
When tragedy strikes, they tell us to fight:
Fight back with a weapon, stay alive, find blame...
But they never say fight for what’s right:
Policy, prevention, peace
For what’s right.
Who is the “they” in José ’s speech? Should a government intervene in
tragic events?
One of the opera’s characters plays the role of a king. What is a king? ES/MS
Over what kind or kinds of government do kings preside? How does a king
compare to a president or a prime minister?
Sophocles, the author of Antigone, lived in the ancient Greek city-state of ES/MS
Athens. What is a city-state? How were ancient Greek city-states
governed? What were the other ancient Greek city-states? Do any modern
countries have city-states nowadays? Why or why not?
Pittsburgh Opera Education 7

Lesson Plan Stems
Would Time to Act be appropriate for a student matinee? What grades? MS/HS
How could teachers prepare students for attending an opera about school
violence? Should students and audience members be prepared and/or pre-
warned about the content of the opera? If so, who would be responsible for
that warning? Should operas have a rating system similar to movies and
television shows?
This opera explores some of the impacts of gun violence, especially in MS/HS
schools. What are the current statistics connected to school shootings and
gun violence in the United States? How do these statistics compare to
other countries? How can regional, state, and federal governments work to
prevent school shootings?
The civil war that led to Sophocles’ play Antigone arose from a dispute MS/HS
between brothers over succession: which brother would rule Thebes in the
absence of their father. What rules does the United States’ government
have to avoid a fight over succession, if the elected President can no
longer serve? What is the order of succession? Are all those positions
elected positions? If the President is incapacitated, what rules are in place
for that situation?
Though Sophocles’ Antigone is thousands of years old, the play can still HS
resonate with today’s issues. Find news articles that parallel issues of this
Greek tragedy. What is meant by the phrase “Greek tragedy”? How do
governments create laws to prevent tragedies?
Gun violence and school shootings are a uniquely American epidemic. HS
Why? Compare laws in other countries with lower gun violence to those in
the United States. What is the responsibility of the government to prevent
gun violence? Explore this website: 17 Facts About Gun Violence And
School Shootings — Sandy Hook Promise How can citizens participate in
making change?

Sophocles, the man who wrote Antigone, a Greek play from long ago, made PreK

Sophocles was raised in an upper-class environment in ancient Greece. ES
What is this idea of class? What is it based on? What does it mean to be

Time to Act is a new opera. Does it cost more to perform a new or a ES

opera’s costs be the same as a play’s? If not, explain why.

Character Themes for Time to Act
Eleanore N. Childs, a Pittsburgh attorney and child advocate, worked with
teachers for more than a decade to research character education and develop the
Heartwood Ethics Curriculum for Children. Heartwood’s life lessons through
literature promote understanding and practice of seven universal character
attributes, which constitute the foundation of community among all people.
The Heartwood Institute defined seven universal character attributes:
• Courage is not only mastering fear; it is the ability to face difficulty or risk with integrity and
honor.
• Loyalty is being faithful to a person, group, or ideal based on understanding that we are all
part of something greater than ourselves.
• Justice is being guided by a balance of truth, reason, and fairness to all.
• Respect is regard for all life on earth. It calls us to value all people and their contributions,
viewpoints, and differences, such as culture, religion, age, race, and ability.
• Hope guides our future through faith and aspiration, helping us to rise above selfishness
and despair.
• Honesty is being truthful and honorable, the foundation for friendship and community.
• Love is deep care and concern for others, encompassing all the attributes. The more love
we give, the richer we become.
Discuss two attributes—loyalty and respect—after reading the synopsis of Time to
Act.
1. Is there such a thing as being too loyal? Consider the far-reaching
consequences of these characters’ actions to themselves, their families, and the
people they love: Antigone, Creon, Alona, and Alona’s brother. To what extent do
these characters owe loyalty to their family members? Social groups? Do they
owe loyalty to their ideals? Do either Antigone or Alona take their loyalties too far?
2. One element of developing respect is to have empathy, the ability to step into
someone’s shoes to understand that person’s perspective. Why does Jose’s
comment about Creon: “Is a broken heart the only way he can understand
someone’s pain?” catch Alona’s attention? Is it possible to develop empathy for
the families of people who commit violent acts? Why might it be important to have
that sense of empathy? How does Alona’s revealing her secret impact the
students’ respect for her? How does it impact their understanding of Antigone?
3. How is facing death a measure of loyalty and respect in both Antigone and Time
to Act? At one point in the opera, Alona questions if her brother would have shot
her had he known she was there, yet she wears his coat; is this an act of
remembrance, an attempt at empathy, or a measure of loyalty? How is Antigone’s
acceptance of her own death her measure of loyalty and respect for her brother?
In each case, is loyalty and respect simply owed, or should it be earned?
